## 1. Summary

[rev] A gap exists in narrow form only. No US streamer, FAST service, kids network, creator studio or faith network runs a hosted, original, scripted comedy block for families with children age 6 to 14 on a fixed night. The literal claim "no one programs a family night" is false: a public broadcaster has run a weekly Friday family movie night since 2017 [S87], and a university network streams a free weekly family sketch comedy [S88]. Neither hosts, neither is original scripted comedy for 6 to 14, and neither builds a multi-show block. The original sentence follows for the record. No US streamer, FAST service, kids network, creator studio or faith network markets a weekly, fixed-night family comedy block. The closest live models sit outside kids media. Great American Family premieres movies on Saturdays at 8 p.m. ET [S38]. Hallmark runs weekend premiere nights with next-day streaming [S39]. Angel Studios releases some series on a fixed weekday and reports 2.61 million paying Guild members [S36][S37]. In kids media, every major player has moved the other way. Netflix, Disney+ and YouTube compete on library depth, playlists and algorithmic feeds. Linear kids cable has collapsed, and Universal Kids went dark in March 2025 [S6]. Warner Bros. Discovery says live-action kids programming is not a focus [S19]. Amazon folded Freevee into Prime Video [S21]. Kidoodle.TV's owner faces receivership [S27]. The precedent searches in section 7 found no current "family night" or "watch together weekly" block. The white space is real. The constraints are also real. COPPA and YouTube's made-for-kids rules remove notifications, comments, live chat and playlists [S3][S4]. An appointment block relies on those features.

## 7a. Tested and stopped: streaming-era two-generation nostalgia sitcoms [rev]

The red team (R1-03) found three well-funded tests of the two-generation nostalgia bet on streaming and cable. All three ended. Each revived the expression of an old show (characters, sets, titles). None tested a block, hosts, a fixed night, or original characters. They test the H3 audience bet and the H2 "shows set the ceiling" reading, not the wrapper.

Reading. The nostalgia hook buys a premiere. Retention depends on the show and on the child staying. This matches the H2 verdict: the wrapper raises the floor, the show sets the ceiling. It also warns against the master prompt's original H3 framing: two generations show up on night one, and the second generation decides night four. The Phase 5 primary metric (week 4 household return rate) is the right test for exactly this failure mode. Whether the gap is opportunity or graveyard is therefore undecided by history. All three cases borrowed expression from old shows. None built original characters for today's 6 to 14 year olds. Porchlight does the opposite by constraint (Section 3.1 of the master prompt). That difference is a hypothesis, not a finding.

## 8. White space: what nobody does

- No US kids or family service runs a hosted, fixed-night, weekly, original scripted comedy block for ages 6 to 14 [rev: narrowed]. A public broadcaster runs a weekly family movie night without hosts or comedy. Faith networks do fixed nights for movies.
- No streamer offers a native family watch-together feature after Disney+ dropped GroupWatch [S32].
- No kids FAST channel schedules against a clock in a way it markets. All loop 24/7 [S26][S28].
- No brand-funded family app (Chick-fil-A Play) uses a weekly cadence [S68].
- No creator studio (Moonbug, pocket.watch, Dhar Mann) sells an appointment format. All sell libraries and batch orders [S73][S76][S77].
- No one combines Angel's member voting with a kids comedy slate [S36].
- No linear kids network runs a live weekly family event. Nick's CBS simulcast is a single annual night [S17].
- No one pairs a broadcast-style host with made-for-kids YouTube distribution, because the rules disable the host's engagement tools [S3].

## 9. Constraints that shape the product

COPPA (2025 amendments). The amended rule took effect June 23, 2025, with most compliance due April 22, 2026 [S86]. It adds biometric identifiers to personal information [S86]. It requires separate parental consent for non-integral disclosures [S86]. It requires written security and retention programs [S86]. Behavioral targeting of under-13 viewers is off the table on any platform.

YouTube made-for-kids designation. A block aimed at ages 6 to 14 with kid-appeal characters is likely child-directed. Setting it made for kids removes comments, notifications, live chat, end screens and cards [S3][S5]. It also removes playlists, Watch Later, posts, stories, memberships, Super Chat and personalized ads [S3]. Monetization drops to contextual ads with bumpers [S4]. The Disney case shows the FTC fines mislabeling, so "not made for kids" is not a safe workaround [S59]. A weekly reminder to return therefore cannot rely on YouTube notifications. It must live in a parent-facing channel or a separate app.

Platform limits. FAST channels loop; a fixed night needs an EPG slot and a promo push the platform controls [S26]. SVOD partners buy batches, not schedules; Ms. Rachel dropped in four-episode sets [S9]. Linear kids cable has few viewers and an uncertain owner. Universal Kids is gone [S6]. Paramount may divest Cartoon Network or Nick to close the WBD deal [S62][S66]. PBS cannot carry ads and has lost funding [S40].

Attention competition. Roblox reports 123M daily users and YouTube holds the largest share of TV viewing [S1][S56]. A fixed night competes with feeds that never end.

Watch-together tooling. No major streamer offers native family co-viewing after 2023 [S32][S33]. A block that wants a shared living-room moment must design for a single screen.
