# Voice

Project Porchlight, Phase 2. Brand strategist output. Internal.
`[NAME]` stands for the brand name until Fork D closes. The recommendation in `names-5.md` is Streetlights.

## Voice in one line

A confident host who is glad you came. Warm, plain, funny on purpose, never winking.

## Voice rules (10)

1. Speak to the parent. Never call a child to act. No "click," "subscribe," "share," or "tell us" aimed at a kid (`canon/STANDARDS.md` section 2, item 10).
2. Short sentences. One idea each. If a sentence needs a comma to breathe, split it.
3. Say the night. Every public page names the fixed night and the block. The reader should never wonder when.
4. Be warm without being soft. No "cozy," "snuggle," "magical," or "wholesome." Show the couch. Do not describe the feeling of the couch.
5. Be confident without being loud. No exclamation points in headings. One per page at most in body copy.
6. Never wink. No irony about being a TV block, no "remember when," no quotation marks around the word retro. The guide format is played straight.
7. Never name the old blocks. No use of the two block trademarks named in `canon/STANDARDS.md` section 2, item 13, and no reference to any 1990s brand, jingle, or catchphrase in any public text (`canon/STANDARDS.md` section 2, items 12 and 13).
8. Jokes land for a 35-year-old and stay clean for a 6-year-old. If a line needs the kid not to get it, cut it.
9. State facts with a source or not at all. Audience numbers, results, and claims about the old blocks stay in research files.
10. No em dashes. Restructure the sentence. Spell out acronyms on first use.

## Ritual language

One word per concept. Internal words from `canon/GLOSSARY.md` stay as they are. This table adds the public word a family says.

| Concept | Internal word | Public word | How it sounds |
|---|---|---|---|
| The fixed night | Fixed night | [NAME] Night | "It's [NAME] Night." |
| The full program | Block | The block | "Tonight's block starts at the top of the hour." |
| Slot 1 | Anchor | Anchor | Printed as the first listing. "First up, the Anchor." |
| Slot 2 | Hangout | Hangout | "Then the Hangout." |
| Slot 3 | Wildcard | Wildcard | "Then the Wildcard. Anything goes." |
| Slot 4 | Lights Out | Lights Out | "And last, Lights Out. Then bed." |
| The host segments | Interstitial, home base | The Wrap | "Stay for the Wrap." The hosts speak from Home Base. |
| The transition | Bumper | Bumper | Not spoken. Seen and heard. |
| The after-show | Ritual kit | The Last Word | One question, one snack card, one thing to do. "Tonight's Last Word is on the fridge." |
| The reminder | Parent email | The Listing | "The Listing lands in your inbox before the block." |
| The sign-up | Parent account | Save the night | "Save the night" is the button. The parent is the account holder. |
| The vote | Metric | I'd watch this | The one thing a parent can say about a trailer. |
| The premiere | Premiere | Premiere | "Premieres on [NAME] Night." |

Rules for the ritual words:
- The public word never replaces the internal word in `canon/` or in agent files.
- "Lights Out" is Slot 4 only. It never means bedtime in copy, so the two ideas do not blur.
- "The Wrap" is the host moment. Not "the bit," not "the segment."
- The child is never named as the user. "Your kid" and "the whole house" are the words. Never "you" to a child.

## The promise to parents

One night a week, one couch, one thing you all laugh at, and nothing you have to apologize for.

## The promise to kids

Something that is yours every week, with a little scare at the end and a safe landing.

The promise to kids is spoken by a host on screen. It is never written as a call to action to a child.

## Six sample lines

1. Hero line (site home): "One night a week. Four new shows. The whole house on one couch."
2. Guide tagline (under the masthead): "[NAME]. The block premieres every [fixed night]. Listings inside."
3. Trailer page prompt (above the player, addressed to the parent): "Watch the trailer. Then tell us one thing: would your house watch this?"
4. Email subject (The Listing): "Tonight's block: the Anchor, the Hangout, the Wildcard, and Lights Out"
5. Vote button label: "I'd watch this"
6. Footer line: "[NAME] is for families. A parent holds every account. We collect nothing from children. Read how we handle data."

Each sample line names the parent as the actor and the household as the audience. The footer line satisfies `canon/STANDARDS.md` section 4, item 2.
