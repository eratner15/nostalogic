# Position

Project Porchlight, Phase 2. Brand strategist output. Internal.
Every fact cites `canon/AUDIENCE.md` (A) or `deliverables/01-gap-memo.html` (G) by section. No other sources are used here.

## One line

An original comedy block for the whole house, on one fixed night a week, made new for the kids of the parents who grew up on one.

## For whom

The household. One parent account plus the people who watch with it (A, section 1).

The parent:
- Born 1981 to 1990. Age 36 to 45 in 2026 (A, section 2).
- Watched a fixed-night family comedy block as a child. Recalls the night, the hosts, the late bedtime, and the jingle more than any one plot (A, section 2).
- Already co-views. Over 90 percent of parents of children 2 to 12 co-view (A, section 1).
- Feels behind on screen time. 42 percent say they could manage it better. 65 percent say they spend too much time on their own phone (A, section 2).
- Names nostalgia as a driver of shared content time (A, section 2).
- Holds the account, the remote, and the decision about the night (A, section 2).

The child:
- Age 6 to 14, in two bands. Ages 6 to 9 watch with a parent almost always. Ages 10 to 14 start to pull toward feeds and games (A, section 3).
- Wants something that feels like theirs, characters to talk about at school, and a mild scare with a safe landing (A, section 3).
- Cannot hold an account, comment, chat, or receive a notification. The product never asks the child for anything (A, section 3).

The parent's jobs the brand must serve (A, section 2):
1. One night a week where the whole family sits on one couch and laughs at the same thing.
2. A screen ritual the parent is proud of, not one the parent apologizes for.
3. A way to share a piece of the parent's own childhood without showing the kid a 30-year-old rerun.
4. Make it easy. Tell me when, tell me what, and give me something to do after.

## Against what

The brand competes for a night, not for a library.

Against the feed and the playground:
- YouTube holds 13.8 percent of all TV viewing. Roblox has 123 million daily users. Netflix counted kids as nearly 22 percent of 2025 views. They own the feed, the library, or the playground. None owns a night for ages 6 to 14 (G, section 3).
- Only 17 percent of parents co-view short-form video. The feed is watched alone (A, section 2).

Against the closest live models:
- A public broadcaster runs a weekly family movie night with no hosts and no original comedy, aimed at preschool and early elementary (G, section 3; A, section 5).
- A faith-and-family cable network premieres movies on a fixed night. A greeting-card network runs weekend premiere nights. A values-based studio releases an animated series weekly to 2.61 million paying members (G, section 3).
- None of these runs a hosted, original, scripted comedy block for families with children 6 to 14 on a fixed night (G, section 1, H1; G, section 3).

Against the rerun:
- Three streaming-era nostalgia sitcoms drew both generations on night one and lost the child by the third season (A, section 4). The brand is not a revival. It is new characters for today's kids, with the parent's ritual as the wrapper.

Against nothing at all:
- No source measures parent demand for a scheduled weekly ritual (A, section 5). The brand's first job is to make that demand visible: the trailer site, the votes, and the parent sign-ups (G, section 8; D-013 in `DECISIONS.md`).

## Why now

1. The audience math lines up. 33.3 million US families have children under 18. Parents of today's 6 to 14 year olds were born mostly in the 1980s. That is the generation that watched a fixed-night comedy block as children (G, section 1, H3a; A, section 2).
2. The supply gap is real. No US streamer, FAST service, kids network, creator studio, or faith network runs a hosted, original, scripted comedy block for families on a fixed night (G, section 1, H1; G, section 3).
3. The old blocks ended on cost and strategy, not on the end of demand (G, section 1, H4).
4. Kids media is contracting. One kids cable channel went dark in March 2025. One family cable channel closed in August 2025. The largest family streamer retired watch-together in 2023 (G, section 3). Fewer incumbents hold the night.
5. Appointment viewing survives on streaming. Weekly drops create watercooler moments (G, section 2, H1).
6. Made-for-kids platform rules disable comments, notifications, and playlists. The weekly reminder must live in a parent-facing channel. That makes the owned site the product, not a marketing page (G, section 4; G, section 9).
7. A small studio can prove audience before capital. Nine creator cases show it (G, section 1, H5).

## What the brand must not claim

- It must not claim that a 30-year nostalgia cycle drives timing. That hypothesis is not supported (G, section 1, H3b).
- It must not claim parent demand for a weekly ritual until the demand test measures it (A, section 5).
- It must not claim to serve ages 13 to 14 with co-viewing data. No source measures that band (A, section 2).

## Position statement

For parents born in the 1980s who want one night a week on one couch with their kids, [NAME] is the original comedy block that premieres on a fixed night, with new characters for today's 6 to 14 year olds and the ritual the parent remembers. Unlike the feed, the library, and the rerun, it owns a night, not a scroll.
