# KITCHEN COURT: Character and set sheet

For the image generation model (trailer plates and inserts under D-014 and CG-03) and for casting (CG-04).
Source: `projects/04-writers-room/out/bibles/A2-kitchen-court.md` sections 2, 3, and 11.

Rules for anyone using this sheet:

1. Every person here is an original character. Nobody is modeled on a real actor or a named character from any existing show. Casting notes never say "like" followed by a real person's name. Image prompts never name a real person.
2. No AI likeness of any regular appears in a released asset without CG-03 sign-off and a disclosure. No AI child likeness is requested at all. The image model builds the empty set, the six fixed frames, and the no-face inserts (bible, section 11).
3. Every visible word is listed below. Nothing else on the set carries readable text. No brand, no logo, no label.
4. Light is dusk through the sink window plus the yellow ceiling light. Same light in every frame.
5. The constant pieces per character never change across episodes. Everything else can.

---

## 1. The regulars

### Renata Delacroix, 44. Mother. "Judge Mom."

- **Age and build.** 44. Tall, long-limbed, straight-backed. She takes up vertical space and knows it.
- **Face.** Oval. High forehead, strong brow, wide mouth that rests in a half-smile as if she is waiting for the end of your sentence. A small mole above the left eyebrow. Eyes dark brown, steady.
- **Hair.** Dark brown, tightly coiled, pulled back into a low twist and held with a plain band. Reading glasses live pushed up into the hair, always, even when she is reading.
- **Skin.** Deep brown, warm undertone.
- **Constant costume.** A work blazer, navy or charcoal, over a plain crew-neck top. The blazer comes off by act two and hangs on the back of her chair. A thin gold watch on the left wrist. Flat loafers. Trousers, never a skirt.
- **The one prop.** The reading glasses pushed up in her hair. She pulls them down only to read a ruling from paper.
- **Posture.** Sits the way she sits downtown: feet flat, hands folded on the table, chin level. On the Stool she keeps her knees together and her back straight and is a full head above everyone, which she knows looks ridiculous.
- **Fixed frame.** F2, Stool Single: low angle from the gallery side, looking up at her on the top step, the fridge soft behind. Off the Stool she sits in the near chair closest to the head, right of center, turned three-quarters to camera. When she is a party under Rule Four she sits on the gallery bench, center, between her children, and looks small there.

### Wendell Huang, 45. Father. "Facilities."

- **Age and build.** 45. Medium height, broad shoulders, thick forearms, a soft middle. Built by carrying chairs.
- **Face.** Round and warm. Heavy eyelids. Deep smile lines. A small chip in one front tooth from a rental accident he tells differently every time. Eyes dark, kind, a little tired.
- **Hair.** Black going gray at the temples, cut short, flattened on one side from a cap he has just taken off.
- **Skin.** Light warm tan.
- **Constant costume.** A faded green work polo. Over the pocket, small, stitched: HUANG PARTY RENTAL. Cargo shorts in every season. White sneakers with one black scuff. A canvas apron when he is at the range. A cap in his back pocket.
- **The one prop.** A brown clipboard with a metal clip and a pen on a string. It lives in his back pocket or his hand.
- **Posture.** Never fully sits. Perches on the arm of his chair, leans on the counter, keeps one foot ready. Enters every scene carrying something. Sits properly only when Ref tells him to.
- **Fixed frame.** F1, Table Wide: the chair with arms at the left end of the table, frame left, nearest the fridge and the garage door traffic. His single is a slow pan from F1, never a new setup.

### Antoinette "Toni" Delacroix, 73. Grandmother. "Ref."

- **Age and build.** 73. Small, wiry, narrow shoulders, stands like a dancer. Weight on the balls of her feet.
- **Face.** Heart-shaped. Sharp cheekbones. Deep-set eyes. Thin lips. One eyebrow rides higher than the other, permanently. Lines at the eyes from forty years of squinting at a court.
- **Hair.** Silver-white, cropped close to the head.
- **Skin.** Deep brown, matte, with the lines earned.
- **Constant costume.** A black zip track jacket with two white stripes down each sleeve, zipped to the chest. A white crew-neck under it. Gray track pants with a crease. White court sneakers, spotless. A red lanyard around her neck.
- **The one prop.** A brass pea whistle on the red lanyard. Old, dull, worn bright where her thumb goes. It rises into frame before she does.
- **Posture.** Referee stance: feet shoulder-width, hands loose and ready to signal. She never touches the table. She never touches evidence. Her signals are real basketball signals repurposed: fists rolling for Traveling, a slap of the wrist for Hands, a wrist grab for Holding, a hand on the hip for Offside, a wind-up of one arm for Delay of game.
- **Fixed frame.** F1, far right, at the stair rail: the whistle rises into frame, then her face, waist up. At the table she takes the middle near chair and turns it sideways to the table so her left profile is to camera, treating the table as a sideline. Her calls are always played in F1 wide so the whole signal reads.

### Marisol Delacroix-Huang, 13. Eldest. "Counsel."

- **Age and build.** 13. Tall for her age, all elbows, mid-growth, not yet used to her own reach.
- **Face.** Long. Her mother's brow, her father's round chin. Braces, unhidden, and she does not cover her mouth. A serious resting face that breaks fast when she forgets to hold it.
- **Hair.** Dark brown, loose curls to the shoulder, held back with two plain black clips, one always slipping.
- **Skin.** Medium brown, warm.
- **Constant costume.** A maroon school cardigan with the sleeves pushed to the elbow. A white T-shirt. Jeans. Mismatched socks, one always striped. A pencil behind the right ear.
- **The one prop.** The folder: a fat white file folder with colored tabs, the edge labeled in marker with whatever the case is. There is a separate, thicker folder for her brother.
- **Posture.** Sits forward with forearms on the table and the folder open. Stands to speak, one hand on the folder. Sits down the moment she asks about a sleepover.
- **Fixed frame.** F3, Cross-Table Two-Shot: the gallery bench, camera left position, the side nearest the fridge. Her single is a push-in from F3.

### Ansel Delacroix-Huang, 10. Middle. "The Filer."

- **Age and build.** 10. Small, compact, restless, a leg always bouncing under the table.
- **Face.** Round. Big dark eyes. Expressive eyebrows that do most of his acting. A gap between the front teeth. Everything he feels is on his face at once, and he is usually feeling three things.
- **Hair.** Black, thick, straight, cut short, with a cowlick at the crown that stands up no matter what.
- **Skin.** Light tan.
- **Constant costume.** One clip-on tie, navy with small yellow dots, worn over any plain T-shirt. Shorts. Sneakers with hook-and-loop straps. The tie is on the moment there is a case and off the moment there is not.
- **The one prop.** The tie. He straightens it before he speaks and after he loses.
- **Posture.** Stands to speak with one hand flat on the table, a gesture he saw once and kept. Sits down too fast. Slides a plate of evidence across the table with two fingers.
- **Fixed frame.** F3, Cross-Table Two-Shot: the gallery bench, camera right position, the side nearest the garage door. On the Stool (rare) he is F2 and he freezes.

### Philippa "Pip" Delacroix-Huang, 6. Youngest. "Bailiff."

- **Age and build.** 6. Small, sturdy, square shoulders. Stands like a fence post.
- **Face.** Round. A chin she juts when she announces. Straight, thick brows. Wide-set dark eyes that do not blink when she is counting. A missing top front tooth, left side.
- **Hair.** Dark brown, in two low puffs tied with blue bands.
- **Skin.** Medium brown.
- **Constant costume.** The sash: a blue-and-white striped tea towel folded lengthwise, worn from the right shoulder to the left hip, pinned at the shoulder with one large safety pin. Under it, a yellow long-sleeve top. Leggings. Rain boots, indoors, always, any weather.
- **The one prop.** The sash. When it comes off, something has changed. Her hand on the toaster lever is the secondary image.
- **Posture.** At attention beside the Cart, one hand resting on the toaster lever, eyes on the speaker's mouth. On the Stool she stands on the second step with both hands on the Masher, because sitting would put her below the fruit bowl.
- **Fixed frame.** F5, Cart Insert: tight on the toaster, the lever, her hand, face optional. When she presides, F2, Stool Single, set lower than for her mother, with the fruit bowl slid to the edge of frame. Off duty she sits on the gallery bench between her brother and sister.

## 2. The set

### The kitchen

One room. A split-level kitchen at the back of a house, an addition by a previous owner, larger than the living room. Warm, worn, crowded, clean.

- **Walls and light.** Paper-bag tan walls. A green ceramic tile backsplash, four-inch squares, some crazed with age. One yellow glass ceiling light on a pull chain, the chain unused. Dusk light through the window over the sink. Nothing on the walls except what the bible lists.
- **Back wall, left.** An off-white refrigerator older than the youngest child, its freezer door covered in magnets (see the Docket board below). Left of the freezer, on the fridge body: the Rota, three name magnets on a ring, MARISOL, ANSEL, PIP, with a red arrow magnet. Right of the freezer: five index cards under a pineapple magnet, the Fridge Rules, handwritten.
- **Back wall, center.** The back door, top half glass, a short curtain on a rod. Through it, a backyard at dusk.
- **Back wall, right.** The door to the garage, always open a hand's width. Through the gap: folding chairs stacked to the ceiling, a podium, a giant cardboard number. The inventory changes every week and carries no brand.
- **Right wall.** The split-level stairs. A half-flight up to bedrooms, a half-flight down to the den. A wooden half-wall with a rail. A person coming up appears from the waist up. A person going down disappears from the head down.
- **Left wall.** A white four-burner range and a laminate counter. Open shelves above: mismatched mugs, a fat white three-ring binder with HOUSE in marker on the spine, and a pothos plant in a terracotta pot. A nail in the wall beside the range, for the Masher.
- **Camera wall.** The sink under a window, a wooden dish rack, a dish towel on the oven handle. Seen only in F6, the Sink Reverse.
- **Center.** An oval laminate table, long axis left to right, wood-grain top, chrome legs, a rental tag still stapled under the edge. On the far side, the gallery bench. On the near side, three mismatched wooden chairs. At the left end, one chair with arms. At the right end, the head, nothing, until the Stool arrives. A wooden cutting board lives at the head. A fruit bowl lives at the head too, until Pip presides.
- **Rolling cart.** A metal kitchen cart on casters, two shelves, with a four-slot chrome toaster on top, dial set to 3. Parked by the garage door between cases.
- **The Wobble.** A plain wooden stool with one leg a half-inch short, kept under the counter. The witness seat.
- **Visible text on the set.** DOCKET (black tape, freezer door). HOUSE (binder spine). MARISOL, ANSEL, PIP (Rota magnets). Five handwritten Fridge Rule cards. Case slips in a child's hand. HUANG PARTY RENTAL and `[TOWN]` on the van outside. Nothing else.

### The bench

Two objects answer to "the bench." Both are listed.

**The Stool (the judge's seat).** A red two-step kitchen step stool, enamel over steel, the paint chipped to bare metal on the front edge of both steps. Black rubber feet. A folding handle on the back that does not fold anymore. It lives beside the fridge with its back to the wall. When court opens the bailiff drags it, never carries it, to the head of the table. It scrapes. Whoever presides sits on the top step (Renata, a head above everyone) or stands on the second step (Pip, level with the table after the fruit bowl goes). The Stool at the table means court is in session. The Stool by the fridge means it is just a kitchen. This is the show's on-off switch and the image model should render both states as separate plates.

**The gallery bench (the parties' seat).** A plain wooden bench, the length of the far side of the table, pine, once painted white, worn back to wood on the seat. No back. Three people fit; four on a hard case. It faces camera. The parties sit here, kids' side. When a parent is a party under Rule Four, the parent sits here too, and the camera holds F3 long enough to see it.

### The Docket board

The freezer door of the off-white refrigerator, top half of the fridge, camera-facing, rendered tight in F4 with no faces.

- **Top edge.** A strip of black electrical tape the width of the door. On it, in silver marker, in a child's capitals: DOCKET.
- **Below the tape.** A single column of paper slips, each a torn quarter-sheet, each one line in marker, each held by a wooden clothespin glued to a round magnet. Empty means an empty docket. A full column means a bad week. Slips read like: PIP v. ANSEL. RE: THE LAST WAFFLE. Or: EVERYONE v. MOM.
- **Left of the door, on the fridge body.** The Rota: three white name magnets, MARISOL, ANSEL, PIP, arranged on a ring. A red arrow magnet points at one name. The arrow moves by hand.
- **Right of the door, on the fridge body.** The Fridge Rules: five index cards, one under the other, under a plastic pineapple magnet. Handwritten, three different hands, the oldest card yellowed.
- **Everywhere else.** Ordinary family magnets: a takeout-shaped magnet with no name on it, a child's drawing of a whistle, a school calendar with no school name.

### The Masher

A heavy old wire potato masher. The head is a zigzag of thick steel wire, dulled, with a spot of old rust at one bend. The handle is turned wood, painted red, the paint chipped to bare wood at the grip and at the end where it is tapped. A hole drilled through the end of the handle, with a loop of kitchen string, for the nail. It was Toni's mother's. It hangs on a nail in the wall beside the range, head down. The presiding judge lifts it off the nail, carries it to the head of the table, and taps it twice on the wooden cutting board: to open, to rule, to close. Two taps, never three. Three is a foul. In the F2 frame the Masher sits in the judge's hands, head resting on the board. In the F5-style insert it is the handle, the board, and two taps, no face.

## 3. Notes for the image model

- Build the six frames from the bible, section 11, as empty plates first: F1 Table Wide, F2 Stool Single, F3 Cross-Table Two-Shot, F4 Docket Insert, F5 Cart Insert, F6 Sink Reverse. Same dusk light, same lens height, in every plate.
- Build two states of F1: Stool by the fridge (kitchen) and Stool at the head of the table (court).
- Build the no-face inserts: a slip going under a clothespin magnet, the Rota arrow turning to PIP, the Stool scrape, two taps on the board, toast dropping and popping, the Binder opening, the fruit bowl sliding.
- Do not render any regular's face. Faces are human performances. See rule 2 above.
