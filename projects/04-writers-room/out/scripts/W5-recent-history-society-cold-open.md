Production notes.

1. The Fall is a flash-forward to the reenactment later in the episode. It plays with no context. The Call to Order that follows it is earlier in the meeting.
2. Member Twelve speaks once, over the Fall, as the Society's narrator. Member Twelve is never seen.
3. The bell is mixed at or below the dialogue peak. No music until the title.
4. Falls are tableau falls. Slow, soft, dignified. Nobody is hurt.
5. No em dashes in any on-screen text.

```
FADE IN:

INT. FOUNDRY HALL - THE STAGE - AFTERNOON

SLOW MOTION. A grocery aisle made of cardboard on a low
wooden stage. A sign on a wire: AISLE 5.

A pyramid of painted soup cans, chest high. One can in the
middle is not a can. It is the face of PIPER VOSS, 10, calm
as a lighthouse.

Two cardboard carts roll in from either side, pushed by WREN
and OSCAR, teenagers in bonnets. Behind Oscar's cart, for no
reason anyone will ever give, walks DALE PRUITT, 45, in a
full cloth HORSE HEAD.

                    MEMBER TWELVE (V.O.)
          Tuesday. The market. Four hours
          without eggs. The soup was not
          involved. The soup was nearby.

The carts touch. The pyramid tips. Piper tips with it, arms
open, eyes closing last, into a slow spill of empty cans.

The horse lowers its head in respect.

In row three, MR. PETRAKIS, 68, wakes up.

                    MR. PETRAKIS
          Objection.

                    HARLAN (O.S.)
          So noted.

A BELL rings once.

HARD CUT TO:

INT. FOUNDRY HALL - EARLIER

WIDE. Brick, arched windows, late light. Forty folding
chairs in six rows. Twelve people, spread thin. Above the
stage, a banner: RECENT HISTORY SOCIETY OF BELL HOLLOW.
Under it, repainted many times: EST. RECENTLY.

HARLAN VOSS, 61, bridge tender's cap, pulls the bell rope.

                    HARLAN
          The Recent History Society of
          Bell Hollow is called to order.
          Who remembers?

                    ALL
          We do.

                    HARLAN
          Nominations. One. On Tuesday, at
          approximately nine forty in the
          morning, the market ran out of
          eggs. For four hours. Two carts
          met in aisle five. There were
          witnesses.

Dale raises his hand.

                    DALE
          I was there.

                    HARLAN
          There were witnesses. Two. On
          Friday, at eleven in the morning,
          I returned a library book. On the
          day it was due.

Silence. He has practiced this.

                    HARLAN (CONT'D)
          The town held its breath.

At the panel table, DR. IMANI BEALE, 38, does not look up.

                    IMANI
          The town did not know.

                    HARLAN
          The town will. Vote by program.
          The eggs.

Twelve programs go up, including his own.

                    HARLAN (CONT'D)
          Twelve to nothing. I withdraw the
          library book with dignity.
               (writing, in full)
          The Great Egg Shortage of
          Tuesday.

MRS. LORRAINE TULLIVER, 70, crosses to the costume rack and
lays a hand on the tab marked TUE.

                    MRS. TULLIVER
          Tuesday was a simpler time. Eggs
          on every shelf.

                    HARLAN
          There were no eggs, Lorraine.
          That's the event.

                    MRS. TULLIVER
          Monday, then.

                    HARLAN
          Casting. Wren and Oscar, the
          carts. Harmon and Harmon, the
          shelf. Mr. Petrakis, the store
          manager, when he wakes.

Dale's hand goes up. Straight. Patient. A full beat.

                    DALE
          Is there a horse?

                    HARLAN
          It is a grocery store, Dale.

The hand comes down. Harlan checks the clipboard.

                    HARLAN (CONT'D)
          You are the bakery.

                    DALE
          I'll do my best.

Piper stands. Notebook on a string around her neck.

                    PIPER
          I'd like to be considered for a
          person.

                    HARLAN
          The soup.

                    PIPER
          The soup was in aisle five. The
          eggs were in aisle two. The soup
          didn't see anything.

                    HARLAN
          The soup heard.

                    PIPER
               (writing it down)
          What did it hear?

                    HARLAN
          That is between you and the soup.

                    PIPER
          I'll need a minute.

She takes one painted can to the corner of the stage, sits
with it in her lap, and listens to it.

                    IMANI
               (still not looking up)
          Piper. Don't give the soup a
          family.

                    PIPER
          Too late.

                    HARLAN
          Places for Tuesday.

The room moves. Bonnets from the TUE section. Carts to their
marks. Wren hangs the sign: AISLE 5. Dale stands at the
painted bakery and becomes, visibly, a horse.

Harlan lifts the small brass bell. At the panel table, a RED
CARD goes up. Imani looks at him for the first time.

                    HARLAN (CONT'D)
          Nothing has happened yet.

                    IMANI
          The sign. It was aisle six.

                    HARLAN
          The program is printed.

                    IMANI
          Then the program is wrong.

A long beat. Harlan looks at the sign. The program. The
green filing cabinet by the wall, which offers nothing.

He turns to the room. Chin up.

                    HARLAN
          Who remembers?

                    ALL
          We do.

                    IMANI
          That is not how that works.

Harlan rings the bell.

SMASH CUT TO:

TITLE CARD: RECENT HISTORY SOCIETY

END OF COLD OPEN
```

## Loglines, episodes 2 to 7

Drawn from the Scale Ladder in bible section 4.2. Rung named in brackets.

2. **The Battle of the Four-Way Stop.** [Rung 1, ordinary] Four bicycles reached the corner of Kettle Street in the same second and none of them moved for eleven minutes, so the Society stages the standoff with Dale as all four horses and walks to the real corner to place a cardboard plaque while the neighbors politely ride around it.

3. **The Sweater.** [Rung 2, smaller] Harlan's own sweater return is voted the event of the week, which by the Casting Rule hands the role of Harlan to Piper, who plays him better than he plays himself and leaves the Commodore no choice but to rule "so noted" on his own life.

4. **The Tooth.** [Rung 2, smaller] Piper's tooth came out in the library quiet room, Dale is cast as Piper and asks whether the tooth can be a horse, and the field piece is a whole reenactment performed under the librarian's rule of silence with cards.

5. **The Two Bake Sales.** [Rung 3, disputed] Two members swear a different table sold out first, so two reenactments run on one stage at the same time, and Imani's red card goes up so often the Society votes to retire it for the week, after which she objects with her hand.

6. **The Hailstone.** [Rung 4, unseen] A hailstone the size of a plum hit the back lot with no witness and one jar in the Archive, so the Society reconstructs its fall from the jar alone and Piper, at last, is cast as weather.

7. **The Minutes.** [Rung 5, the Society itself] The Society reenacts its own last meeting, Imani objects to Dale's portrayal of her objection, and the Corrections require Corrections before the bell is allowed to ring.
