```
TIMECODE 00:00    BEAT 1 OF 6: COLD OPEN

INT. FOUNDRY HALL - THE STAGE - AFTERNOON

SLOW MOTION. No context. No title.

A grocery aisle built from cardboard on a low wooden stage.
A hand-lettered sign hangs from a wire: AISLE 5.

From the left, a shopping cart rolls in. From the right,
another. Both are cardboard on wheels, pushed by TWO
TEENAGERS in bonnets, WREN and OSCAR, faces solemn.

Between the carts stands a pyramid of soup cans, painted
cardboard, chest high. In the middle of the pyramid, where
one can should be, is a FACE.

PIPER VOSS, 10. Wearing the cans. Eyes forward. Expression
of a witness who knows what is coming.

Inside the cans, without moving her head, Piper speaks to
the carts.

                    PIPER
          Slower. It was slower than this.

                    OSCAR
          This is as slow as it goes.

The carts meet. Slowly. Front to front. A soft cardboard
kiss.

The pyramid leans. The cans go. Piper goes with them, arms
out, head back, in grief, and lands in a heap of cans that
bounce because they are empty.

She lies still. One can rolls to the edge of the stage and
stops.

A BELL rings once, offscreen.

HARD CUT TO BLACK.


TIMECODE 00:09    BEAT 2 OF 6: WORLD

INT. FOUNDRY HALL - CONTINUOUS

WIDE. A long brick room with tall arched windows and late
light. Forty folding chairs in six rows. Twelve people sit
in them, spread out as if forty were expected.

Above the stage, a cloth banner: RECENT HISTORY SOCIETY OF
BELL HOLLOW. Under it, on a strip that has been repainted
many times: EST. RECENTLY.

At the lectern, HARLAN VOSS, 61, in a bridge tender's cap,
lets go of a bell rope. The bell above him swings.

                    HARLAN
          The Recent History Society of
          Bell Hollow is called to order.
          Who remembers?

                    ALL
          We do.

                    HARLAN
          Last Tuesday, two carts met in
          aisle five. We are here so it is
          not forgotten.

At the panel table, DR. IMANI BEALE, 38, does not look up
from her folder.

                    IMANI
          It was aisle six.

Harlan does not hear it. He has chosen not to.


TIMECODE 00:21    BEAT 3 OF 6: CHARACTER

INT. FOUNDRY HALL - THE COSTUME RACK - MOMENTS LATER

A rolling rack of clothes under seven cardboard tabs: MON,
TUE, WED, THU, FRI, SAT, SUN. Harlan holds a clipboard.
Piper stands before him, at attention, still wearing three
cans.

                    PIPER
          I'd like to be considered for
          the cart.

                    HARLAN
          You're the soup.

                    PIPER
          I was the hydrant last week.

                    HARLAN
          And the neighborhood wept.

DALE PRUITT, 45, mail carrier's shorts, raises his hand.
Holds it there. A full beat of silence.

                    DALE
          Is there a horse?

                    HARLAN
          It's a grocery store, Dale.

                    DALE
          There could be a horse.

Harlan writes something on the clipboard. Dale watches the
pen and hopes.

MRS. LORRAINE TULLIVER, 70, lifts a gray cardigan from the
TUE section and holds it to the light like a relic.

                    MRS. TULLIVER
          Tuesday was a simpler time.

Imani arrives at the rack, folder closed.

                    IMANI
          Harlan. You cannot keep
          reenacting last week.

                    HARLAN
          Those who forget last week are
          doomed to repeat it.

                    IMANI
          That's every week, Harlan.
          That's how weeks work.

Harlan considers this. He writes it down.


TIMECODE 00:44    BEAT 4 OF 6: TURN

INT. FOUNDRY HALL - THE PANEL TABLE - LATER

Three tent cards: HISTORIAN, HISTORIAN, COMMODORE. Three
microphones plugged into nothing. A pitcher of water nobody
has poured.

Imani raises a RED CARD.

                    IMANI
          Point of History. It was aisle
          six. I was standing in aisle
          six. I was buying oats.

                    HARLAN
          Aisle five.

Piper steps forward from the stage, cans and all.

                    PIPER
          It was five. I'm the soup that
          saw everything.

                    IMANI
          You were at school.

A beat. Piper does not blink.

                    PIPER
          I felt it.

Hold on her. Longest hold in the cut.

Imani turns, slowly, and points at the green filing cabinet
with the brass plaque: THE ARCHIVE.

                    IMANI
          The Archive says six.

Harlan looks at the Archive. The Archive does not look back.
He lifts the small brass bell on the lectern.

                    HARLAN
          Sustained. Restage it.


TIMECODE 00:57    BEAT 5 OF 6: BUTTON

INT. FOUNDRY HALL - THE STAGE - LATER

SLOW MOTION. The same cardboard aisle. The same sign, but
the 5 has been crossed out in marker and a 6 written beside
it by hand.

The carts roll in again. Behind the right-hand cart, pushing
it with total dignity, is Dale, in a full cloth HORSE HEAD.
Nobody cast him. Nobody stops him.

At the panel table, Imani reads from a card, to the room,
not to the stage.

                    IMANI
          Aisle six. For the record.

The carts meet. The pyramid goes. Piper falls, slower than
before, with more grief than before, because the Society has
learned from the first one.

She lands. Cans settle. From the floor, to the ceiling:

                    PIPER
          Still felt it.

The horse head turns toward the panel table. Muffled, from
inside the cloth:

                    DALE
          It was seven.

Imani looks at the Archive. The Archive says nothing.

The BELL rings. Harlan, at the lectern, hand on the rope.

                    HARLAN
          Adjourned. Same time next week.
          Something will have happened by
          then.

CUT TO BLACK.


TIMECODE 01:08    BEAT 6 OF 6: TITLE CARD

BLACK. Hand-lettered white text, the same hand as the
banner. No music. Room tone.

                  RECENT HISTORY SOCIETY

                         Wildcard

               Premieres Friday on [BRAND].

          Small line, bottom of frame:
          The account holder is a parent.

TIMECODE 01:13    END OF FILE
```

## The five tests

1. **Question.** "Which aisle was it?" The Turn says six and restages. The Button restages aisle six, and the horse says seven. Imani looks at the Archive, which says nothing. Nobody on screen settles it, and Harlan adjourns on the promise of next week. The question leaves the trailer open.
2. **Two-Laugh.** The 6-year-old laughs on sight at the soup pyramid going over with a kid inside it (00:05) and again at a horse head pushing a cart in a grocery aisle (00:59). The 35-year-old laughs at "That's every week, Harlan. That's how weeks work." (00:41) and at "I was buying oats." (00:46). Neither laugh needs the other.
3. **Cold.** The first nine seconds are a slow-motion fall of a girl dressed as soup who asks the carts to go slower, no narration, no title, no brand, no music. A stranger asks "what is this?" and wants the answer.
4. **Dialogue.** About 55 of 73 seconds are a character speaking to another character on screen, about 75 percent, above the 60 percent floor. Member Twelve has no line in this cut. The booth window stays lit in the hall wide as the character's presence.
5. **Standards.** No real event, place, person, brand, or institution. No child call to action on the title card. No borrowed character, set, catchphrase, prop, or plot. The falls are soft tableau falls with no injury. Generated tableaux carry a CG-03 disclosure; performers carry CG-04. No synthesized voice is needed in this cut.
