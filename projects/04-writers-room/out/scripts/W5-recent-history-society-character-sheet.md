## Character sheet: visual descriptions for image generation and casting

| Gates | Any generated image carries a CG-03 disclosure. Any performer triggers CG-04. The child performer requires the standard protections in that gate. |

Rules for every prompt built from this sheet.

1. Every person here is invented. No prompt may name, reference, or describe a real actor, public figure, or existing character. If a generated face resembles a known person, discard it.
2. No logos, no brand names, no readable text except the signs named in this sheet.
3. No weapons, no blood, no injury, no modern screens. The only glass with light behind it is the sound booth window.
4. The look is a small play, not a documentary. Flat front light plus one warm window side. Camera at the eye height of a seated audience, straight on. No handheld tilt, no lens flare, no fisheye.
5. The cardboard is always visibly cardboard. Paint strokes show. Edges are cut by hand.
6. No em dashes in any text that appears in frame.

The fixed frames are the four compositions every regular is shot in, so the still library matches across characters: the hall (seated or standing among the chairs), the stage (in costume, mid-reenactment), the panel table (the judgment), and the field (the real site in the back lot or the neighborhood).

## 8. The hall set: Foundry Hall

Written as one wide establishing frame, then as parts.

**The wide.** A long brick room, the former pattern shop of a bell foundry. The camera sits at the back, at seated eye height, looking down the room to the stage. Tall arched windows with wired glass run down the left wall and pour late afternoon light across a worn wooden floor with a faded painted center line running the length of the room. Exposed timber rafters overhead. Forty mismatched metal folding chairs in six rows, most gray, three of them a different color (one green, one red, one yellow), set for forty and holding twelve. Front left, a plywood lectern painted navy with a hand-lettered card reading COMMODORE, and above it, hanging from a rafter by a chain, a cast bronze bell the size of a bucket with a pull rope. Front right, a low stage two steps up with a red curtain on a sagging wire that has stopped halfway. Above the stage, a cloth banner: RECENT HISTORY SOCIETY OF BELL HOLLOW, and beneath it a smaller repainted strip: EST. RECENTLY. Stage left, the costume rack. Along the right wall, the panel table and the green filing cabinet. At the very back, up a short flight of wooden stairs, the sound booth window with the light on.

**The stage backdrop.** A hand-painted flat of Bell Hollow: a canal, a lift bridge with its span raised, a market with an awning, a flat-roofed bakery. Naive style, bold outlines, flat color, like a woodcut filled in with house paint. A rectangular slot is cut where the market window is, so a head can appear through it.

**Stage props.** All cardboard, all painted, all on casters or carried: a shopping cart, a four-tier shelf, a park bench, a bus door on a wooden frame, a fire hydrant. Signs hang from wire on cup hooks. Lettering is by hand in black marker.

**The panel table.** A folding table with a white cloth to the floor. Three folding chairs. Three folded tent cards: HISTORIAN, HISTORIAN, COMMODORE. Three silver microphones on desk stands with cables that run off the table and end on the floor, plugged into nothing. A glass pitcher of water with three glasses upside down beside it. A small stack of manila folders. The red card, when not raised, rests on the left folder.

**The back lot, called the field.** Through a gray metal door at the back: a gravel yard behind the hall, a chalk line down the middle, a real red fire hydrant, a wooden picnic table, a flagpole with no flag, and a stack of pallets against a brick wall. Daylight. Weeds at the edges.

**Palette.** Brick red, navy, cardboard tan, bonnet white, the green of the Archive, the red of the card, and the late gold of the windows. Nothing neon.

## 9. The costume rack

A long rolling clothes rack of chrome pipe, sagging slightly in the middle, stage left. Hangers grouped under seven cardboard tabs clipped to the top bar, hand-lettered in black marker: MON, TUE, WED, THU, FRI, SAT, SUN. Each section holds a dozen garments left behind at a dry cleaner over forty years: a gray cardigan, a camel overcoat, a plaid work shirt, a pale blue raincoat, a black wool dress coat, a child's yellow slicker, a wedding jacket in a clear bag. Each garment carries a paper dry cleaner's tag on string. Above the rack, a wooden shelf of hats: bonnets in a row, a tricorn cap, a straw boater, a knit cap, a hard hat, a crown made of cardboard. Below the rack, a plastic bin with HORSE painted on the side in large letters, from which one yarn mane spills. A tall mirror leans against the wall beside the rack, taped at one corner. A folding chair sits beside it, and that is Mrs. Tulliver's chair.

## 10. The Archive

A four-drawer green steel filing cabinet against the right wall, dented on one side, with a small brass plaque screwed to the top drawer that reads THE ARCHIVE. Each drawer has a paper label in a metal slot, hand-lettered by weekday range. The top drawer stands open in most stills, showing manila folders with tabs, and, resting on top of the folders: a bus transfer, a movie ticket stub with no readable text, a faded instant photograph of a puddle, a single mitten, a paper program from a previous meeting. Beside the cabinet, on the floor, a small white mini fridge with a jar visible through its open door, and inside the jar, a hailstone the size of a plum. Above the cabinet, a framed page of the bylaws, typed, too small to read. The cabinet is lit from the window side so the green steel shows its scratches. It is the most important object in the room and it looks like a filing cabinet.

## 11. Tableau style for the reenactments

Every reenactment is built from held frames. The still library is the show's grammar. Build each tableau by these rules.

1. **Composition.** Straight on, from the seated eye height of the third row. The stage fills the lower two-thirds of the frame. The banner and the hanging bell are visible above. No angles from the wings, no overhead, no handheld tilt.
2. **Light.** Flat warm front light plus one cool side from the arched windows. Long soft shadows on the painted backdrop. No spotlights, no color gels, no haze.
3. **Poses.** Every performer holds a full-body pose at the exact moment of the event: carts an inch apart, cans mid-tip, a hat an inch above a head, a foot an inch above a puddle. Faces are dead serious. Nobody smiles. Nobody looks at the camera.
4. **The object.** Piper's object is always dead center, face visible, eyes open, sneakers showing.
5. **The horse.** Somewhere in every tableau, whether or not the event had one, there is a horse, and it is placed with dignity, usually behind a cart, head lowered.
6. **The cardboard.** Props read as cardboard. Brush strokes visible. Cut edges rough. Signs hand-lettered. A tableau that looks expensive is wrong.
7. **The costume.** Every performer wears one garment from the weekday section and one bonnet or hat. Modern clothes show underneath. Mrs. Tulliver's tags are still on the garments.
8. **The program overlay.** For one second before each reenactment, a printed program page: cream paper, a typed title in capitals, "A Tuesday" as a subtitle, a cast list, and a single woodcut-style illustration of the event's key object in black ink (a cart, a shelf, a hydrant, a hailstone).
9. **Slow motion.** Moving tableaux are the same frames in slow motion: a fall, a roll, a hat leaving a head. Soft landings. Cans that bounce. Nobody hurt. The soup gets up.
10. **The Corrections frame.** The closing still of each episode: Imani at the panel table reading from a folder, Harlan's hand on the bell rope, Piper front row far left, Dale's hand half raised, Mrs. Tulliver beside the rack, and the sound booth light on above them all.
