# Change notes. Punch-up pass on the four trailers and four cold opens.

Project Porchlight, Phase 4. Punch-up agent output.
Inputs: the eight v1 scripts in this folder, the four bibles in `../bibles/`, `canon/STANDARDS.md`, `projects/02-brand/out/voice.md` rule 8, and `projects/03-block-format/out/block-bible.md` section 8.
Outputs: eight `-v2.md` files beside the originals, and this file.

## Rules this pass followed

1. Every shot, beat, timecode, character, location, and action is unchanged. The v2 files are the v1 files with the listed lines swapped and one header line added under the title. A file diff shows nothing else.
2. No shot was added or removed. No dialogue line was added or removed. Where a beat had slack, an existing line grew by a few words. Where a beat was full, the change is an equal-length swap.
3. Every trailer stays inside its length. The Wildcard trailer sits at its 73-second target with a 75-second ceiling, so it took the fewest changes.
4. Every last line stands as written. None was beaten.
5. No reference to a real show, actor, brand, or event was added. The receipts joke stays with The Returns Desk.
6. No em dash in any output. Neither block trademark appears in any output.
7. Every revised script was walked against `canon/STANDARDS.md` section 2, items 1 to 15, after the changes. Each passes on my read. The `standards` agent's pass is still required.

Reason codes: JD (joke density), CV (character voice), CL (clarity), RH (rhythm).

## 1. `A2-kitchen-court-trailer-v2.md` (Anchor, 90 seconds). 4 changes.

1. Pip, World beat, about 00:24.
   Before: "Kitchen Court is open. Feet on the floor. Hands on the table."
   After: "Kitchen Court is open. Feet on the floor. Hands on the table. Mom's feet don't count."
   Reason: JD, CV. Renata is standing on a stool when Pip calls "feet on the floor." Pip is a literalist with authority, so she closes the loophole herself. A 6-year-old sees the feet and laughs. The World beat carried 11 seconds of speech in 18, so the four words fit.
2. Toni, World beat, about 00:19.
   Before: "Five dollars or five minutes. Anything bigger goes to the table. It's on the fridge."
   After: "Five dollars or five minutes. Anything bigger goes to the table. It's on the fridge. Laminated."
   Reason: JD, CV. One word, flat and certain, in the scoreboard voice the bible gives her. A parent knows the grandmother who laminates.
3. Marisol, Character beat, about 00:31.
   Before: "I didn't bite the waffle. I have a folder that says so."
   After: "I didn't bite the waffle. I have a folder that says so. It's tabbed."
   Reason: JD, CV. The bible says Marisol over-prepares and sounds like a work email. Two words make the folder funnier and set up Ansel's "She has a folder."
4. Wendell, Character beat, about 00:44.
   Before: "Can we not split it? We could split it. It's a waffle. It comes with lines."
   After: "Can we not split it? We could split it. It's a waffle. The lines are a hint."
   Reason: JD, RH. Same length. "It comes with lines" was a description. "The lines are a hint" is a joke, and it gives the five-voice "No." a sharper thing to land on.

Already tight and left alone: the cold open, "Objection. To what? She has a folder," the 0 and 4 line, "Alone. With the waffle," "I would like a lawyer," and the whole button.

Two-Laugh verdict: PASS. Parent laugh at 00:38 (0 and 4, one of those was a first-grader) and now also at 00:19 ("Laminated"). Kid laugh on sight at 00:09 (toast pops, "Time") and now also at 00:24 ("Mom's feet don't count"). Neither depends on the other.

Standards read: section 2, items 1 to 15, walked after the changes. None triggered. Section 3: soft rule 2, "Mom's feet don't count" is procedure, not contempt, and Renata is climbing the Stool by her own choice. PASS on my read.

Already tight and left alone: "I've had this dream," "Is that a question or a filing?", "Somebody turned four," "Different hands," "That's not a day. That's a motion," Ansel's confession, "That's ten. Table," and the sash coming off. This script needed little.
