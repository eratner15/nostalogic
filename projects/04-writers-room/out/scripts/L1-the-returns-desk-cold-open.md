# The Returns Desk. Episode 101, "Two Receipts." Cold open.

Slot 4, Lights Out. Staff-writer output against `projects/04-writers-room/out/bibles/L1-the-returns-desk.md` sections 3, 4, and 7. Three pages. Episode 1's cold open runs longer than the format's one minute by design: the bible asks that the rules of the desk and Nieves's supervision be stated on screen in this episode. Every later cold open runs under one minute.

Format notes.

- Every effect is mixed at or below the dialogue peak: door bell, ice drop, doorbell chime, dropped boxes.
- The chime's indicator glows once per ring. No other blinking light. No strobe.
- Nieves never leaves the desk area. Marguerite is in every frame with her. Dolores is on the intercom and behind the porthole.
- The fair clue for the cold open's mini-turn (the chime has its own batteries) is on screen, printed on the chime in Setup B, before Nieves reads it aloud. The pilot's main cause (a transmitter across the street) is not revealed here. Its clue is planted in the insert in Act One.

```
                        THE RETURNS DESK

                   Episode 101. "Two Receipts."

                           COLD OPEN


FADE IN:

INT. HARDISON'S HARDWARE, RETURNS DESK, NIGHT

SETUP A. The gooseneck lamp is the warmest thing in the
room. MARGUERITE SALCEDO, 63, writes a manila tag in a
slow hand. Behind her, THE SHELF OF RETURNED THINGS: a
timer, a rain gauge, a rake, a bath plug, each on a
paper tag. At the far end of the counter, INES RUIZ, 12,
does a math worksheet the way other people do a
crossword. Frame right, a SWINGING DOOR with a porthole.
A shadow crosses it. The dock INTERCOM clicks.

                    DOLORES (V.O.)
              (through the intercom)
          Nieves. Desk?

                    INES
          Desk.

The intercom clicks off. TEDDY VANCE, 16, crosses behind
them with a push broom, headed upstage to AISLE 12, where
the overheads stop and the paint cans go on into the dark.

                    TEDDY
              (to himself)
          Aisle 12 is paint. Paint is a
          liquid. Liquids sit there.

He goes in. His flashlight beam goes with him, slow.

The FRONT DOOR BELL rings at talking volume. DESMOND
NUNES, 41, comes in out of the dark in a raincoat over a
pajama top, carrying a shoebox in both hands the way you
carry a pie.

                    MARGUERITE
              (not looking up)
          Returns?

He sets the box beside the service bell and lifts the
lid. SETUP B: a white WIRELESS DOORBELL CHIME and its
BUTTON, taped together with painter's tape like
something that might get away.

                    MARGUERITE (O.S.)
          What does it do.

                    DESMOND (O.S.)
          It rings.

                    MARGUERITE (O.S.)
          They do.

                    DESMOND (O.S.)
          When nobody's there.

SETUP A. Marguerite's pen stops. That is all it does.

                    MARGUERITE
          Receipt?

He hands over a receipt folded into eighths. She
unfolds it flat with both hands and reads every line.

                    MARGUERITE (CONT'D)
          This is ours. Store policy says
          receipt required. My policy says
          story required. Start where you
          were standing.

Nieves shuts the worksheet, opens a green cloth LEDGER,
and uncaps a pen. Four columns: ITEM. CLAIM. CLUE. CAUSE.

                    INES
          Item. Doorbell, wireless. Claim?

                    DESMOND
          Who's that?

                    MARGUERITE
          Ledger.

                    INES
          I'm the ledger. One sentence.

                    DESMOND
          It rings when nobody is on the
          porch. Eleven times tonight. I
          looked through the peephole. I
          looked through the window. I looked
          under the mat.

                    MARGUERITE
          Good. That's thorough. Yours is a
          good one.
              (holds up the receipt)
          Every return with a story needs two
          receipts. This is one. The other is
          a thing I can check.

                    DESMOND
          It won't ring here. I took the
          battery out.

He sets a COIN BATTERY on the counter, like evidence.

                    INES
              (writing under CLUE)
          Battery removed.

                    MARGUERITE
          Then it's not haunted here. Then
          it's a doorbell. Then I can process
          it.

She takes the RETURNED stamp from the tray, inks it, and
raises it over the form. Holds it there.

SETUP B. The chime under the lamp. Printed on its face,
in focus: CHIME UNIT: 2 x AA INCLUDED. BUTTON: 1 x COIN
CELL. The indicator glows once. The DOORBELL RINGS. Two
notes. No louder than anyone in the room.

SETUP A. Nobody moves. The stamp stays in the air. In
Aisle 12, a case of light bulbs meets the floor, softly.
Teddy backs out of the aisle, broom held in front of him
like a railing, until he is standing next to Nieves.

                    TEDDY
          Okay. Okay. Okay. That wasn't ice.

Nieves looks at the coin battery. Then at the chime. She
leans over it without touching it and reads.

                    INES
          Chime unit. Two double-A. Button.
          One coin cell.

                    DESMOND
          Those are two different things.

                    INES
          Those are two different things.

                    DESMOND
          So it can ring. Then why is it
          ringing?

Silence. Nieves has nothing. She hates having nothing.

                    INES
              (writing under CAUSE)
          Pending.

                    MARGUERITE
              (slowly, to the chime)
          It rang at your house. Now it's
          ringing at mine. Thirty-one years
          at this counter. Nothing has ever
          once rung in here.

She lowers the RETURNED stamp, unused. She takes a fresh
tag and writes: DOORBELL. CLAIM: RINGS. Her pen stops.

                    INES
          You always put something for cause.

                    MARGUERITE
          Then this is new.

The ICE MACHINE by the front door drops a load, a bucket
of gravel on a tin roof, no louder than anyone has
spoken. Teddy jumps.

                    TEDDY
          I know it's ice. But now there's
          two things.

                    MARGUERITE
              (to Desmond)
          Sign there.

                    DESMOND
          What am I signing?

                    MARGUERITE
          That I heard it.

He signs. She brings the hand-cut stamp down on the form:
STORY HEARD. On the down beat, the DOORBELL RINGS.
Marguerite does not look at it. She looks at Nieves.

                    MARGUERITE (CONT'D)
          Underline "here."

                                            SMASH CUT TO:

                        THE RETURNS DESK

                        END OF COLD OPEN
```

## Six loglines. Season 1, episodes 2 to 7.

2. **As Installed.** A grandmother returns a stepladder that is one rung taller every morning, and Nieves traces the extra rungs to a grandfather building his grandson a way up to the treehouse at night without wanting the credit, which she files under kindness and hates.
3. **Aisle 12.** Teddy returns his own flashlight for showing him eyes at the dark end of the paint aisle, Marguerite walks him down while Nieves works the geometry from the desk, and the eyes turn out to be reflective safety stickers on the top shelf, on the same night Mr. Quayle returns a rake with a receipt for a hose.
4. **Still Air.** A night-shift nurse returns a wind chime that chimes on windless nights, and the desk finds a very patient cat on her porch rail that has been waiting up for her to come home.
5. **Not On The Chart.** A couple returns a paint chip that glows after dark, Teddy confesses he mislabeled the glow primer on his first night, Marguerite processes him as a return with a story, and Nieves notices the blank tag for the first time.
6. **Faces The House.** A widower returns a garden gnome that turns to face his house every night, the desk catches the neighbor's kid doing it because the man once said it looked lonely facing the street, and Dolores threatens to charge the gnome rent.
7. **Wrong Door.** A renter returns a key that opens the wrong door, Nieves proves it opens every door in the building and is the master key a landlord lost in the mailroom a week ago, and when she asks Marguerite about the blank tag to her face, the only answer is "Pending."
