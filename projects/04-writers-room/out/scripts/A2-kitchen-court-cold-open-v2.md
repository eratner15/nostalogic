# KITCHEN COURT: Pilot cold open

v2. Punch-up pass. Every changed line is listed in `change-notes.md` beside this file, with before, after, and reason. Shots, beats, timecodes, characters, locations, and actions are unchanged from v1. Only dialogue lines and the wording of action lines moved.

Episode 101, "The Last Waffle." Anchor slot. Project Porchlight, Phase 4.
Source: `projects/04-writers-room/out/bibles/A2-kitchen-court.md` sections 2, 3, 4, and 7.
Frames F1 to F6 are the fixed frames in the bible, section 11.
The cold open runs about two and a half pages and ends on a hard turn into the title.
`[TOWN]` is an Orchestrator placeholder.

```
                          KITCHEN COURT

                            "The Last Waffle"

                             COLD OPEN

FADE IN:

INT. DELACROIX-HUANG KITCHEN, EVENING

F1, TABLE WIDE. A split-level kitchen, bigger than the living room
it was added onto. Paper-bag tan walls. Green tile. A yellow
ceiling light on a pull chain nobody uses. The garage door, frame
right, stands a hand's width open. Through it: folding chairs
stacked to the ceiling and one giant cardboard number 4.

WENDELL HUANG, 45, works a waffle iron at the range like a man
running a shift. A clipboard sticks out of his back pocket. Six
plates in a row on the counter. Five waffles on five plates. The
iron hisses.

                         WENDELL
          Six waffles. Six people. Nobody gets
          two. Nobody gets none. Everybody gets
          a sixth.

PIP, 6, stands at the fridge in a blue-stripe tea towel pinned
across her chest like a sash. She reads the freezer door. A strip
of black tape at the top says DOCKET. Under it: nothing.

                         PIP
               (a weather report)
          The docket is empty.

                         TONI (O.S.)
          Good.

A brass whistle on a red lanyard rises into frame at the top of
the stairs, frame right. TONI DELACROIX, 73, follows it up, one
half-flight at a time.

                         TONI (CONT'D)
          Empty docket. Nobody's hurt. That's a
          clean game.

                         PIP
          It's not a game, Ref.

                         TONI
          Everything's a game. That's why
          there's a ref.

The back door opens. RENATA DELACROIX, 44, work blazer, a canvas
bag on one shoulder, reading glasses pushed up into her hair. She
stops and takes in the room. Five waffles. No slips. The red stool
is by the fridge, where it belongs.

                         RENATA
          Empty docket. Hot waffles. I've had
          this dream.

MARISOL, 13, comes down the stairs behind Toni with a fat white
folder under her arm. She does not sit. She stands at the table
like she has an appointment.

                         MARISOL
          Can Nadia sleep over Friday?

                         RENATA
          Is that a question or a filing?

                         MARISOL
          It's a question. If it needs to be a
          filing it can be a filing. I've got
          the slip.

She has the slip. She holds it up.

                         RENATA
          It's a question. Ask me again when
          there's syrup.

                         MARISOL
          Noted.

She sits on the gallery bench and opens the folder anyway.

ANSEL, 10, comes in from the garage in a clip-on tie over a
T-shirt, carrying the giant cardboard 4 in front of him like a
shield.

                         ANSEL
          Dad, why do we have a four?

                         WENDELL
          Somebody turned four.

                         ANSEL
          Who?

                         WENDELL
          I don't ask. I deliver.

He sets the sixth waffle on the sixth plate.

                         WENDELL (CONT'D)
          That's the six. Plates to the table.
          Facilities is closed.

Plates move. Everyone lands where they always land. Wendell in the
chair with arms at the left end. Toni sideways in the middle chair,
like it's a sideline. Marisol and Ansel on the gallery bench.
Renata in the near chair by the head. Pip's plate goes on the bench
between her brother and sister. Pip herself heads for the sink.

                         TONI
          Hands.

                         PIP
          I'm washing them.

                         TONI
          Different hands. That one's a foul.
          This one's soap. Go.

Pip goes. Water runs. Wendell pours syrup down the line like he is
fueling a fleet.

                         RENATA
          How was everyone's day. One sentence
          each. That's not a rule, I'm just
          tired.

                         MARISOL
          Nadia's mom said yes if you say yes.

                         RENATA
          That's not a day. That's a motion.

                         ANSEL
          I got picked last in gym, but I got
          picked, and I'd like that on the
          record.

                         RENATA
          So noted. Dad?

                         WENDELL
          Forty chairs went out to a retirement
          party. Forty came back. Nobody sat
          down. I don't ask.

Pip comes back, drying her hands on the sash. She stops at the
bench. She looks at her plate.

Her waffle has one bite out of it.

Everyone sees it at the same time. Nobody moves.

F3, CROSS-TABLE TWO-SHOT. Pip and Ansel. Ansel is chewing. He
swallows.

                         ANSEL
          Before anyone says anything.

                         PIP
          Ansel.

                         ANSEL
          I'd like the record to show I did it
          after I asked, and nobody said no.

                         PIP
          Nobody was here.

                         ANSEL
          Nobody said no.

                         WENDELL
          Can we split it? It's a waffle. It's
          got lines. It's the most splittable
          food we own.

                         PIP
          No.

                         ANSEL
          Also, yes, it was me, and I'm sorry,
          and I still think it's fine, because
          it was one bite and there's a lot of
          waffle left, and I'd like all of that
          on the record too.

                         TONI
               (to Renata)
          He's going to talk himself out of it.

                         RENATA
          He's going to talk himself out of it
          and then back into it. He gets that
          from me.

Pip stares at Ansel. She does not blink. Ansel puts his fork down.

                         RENATA (CONT'D)
          Rule One. Is it five dollars or five
          minutes?

                         PIP
          It's a waffle.

                         ANSEL
          It's a five-dollar waffle to her.

                         PIP
          It's a five-minute waffle to me.

                         TONI
          That's ten. Table.

Renata looks at her own waffle. She looks at the red stool by the
fridge. She does the math on her evening.

                         RENATA
          Fine. I'll hear it. I'll be fair. And
          I'll say it in one sentence.

                         TONI
          You've never said hello in one
          sentence.

                         RENATA
          I've never had to rule against one of
          mine. Downtown they're all somebody
          else's.

                         WENDELL
          The plan was the six.

Pip walks to the fridge. She takes a blank slip from under the
pineapple magnet. She writes, tongue out, big letters. She clips it
under DOCKET with a clothespin magnet.

F4, DOCKET INSERT. The slip: PIP v. ANSEL. RE: THE LAST WAFFLE.

F1. Pip turns to the room and reads it aloud the way she reads
everything on that door.

                         PIP
          Pip versus Ansel. Regarding: the last
          waffle.

                         ANSEL
          You can't sue me.

                         PIP
          Why.

                         ANSEL
          You're the bailiff. The bailiff isn't
          on a side. You said. You're on the
          toaster.

Pip looks at the cart. The toaster. Her toaster. She looks down at
the sash.

A long beat, for a six-year-old.

She unpins the tea towel. She folds it once, then twice. She sets
it on the cart next to the toaster, squared to the edge.

                         PIP
          Now I'm on a side.

Silence. Renata looks at the cart. Then at the table.

                         RENATA
          Who runs the toaster?

Every head turns to Toni.

                         TONI
          No.

SMASH CUT TO:

                         KITCHEN COURT

                            END OF COLD OPEN
```

## Season 1 loglines, episodes 2 to 7

2. **Counsel.** Marisol sues for a later bedtime and hires Ref as her lawyer, and Toni, who has spent forty years refusing to take a side, takes one, hates every second of it, and wins.
3. **Six Chairs.** Wendell rents all forty chairs to the block party and forgets to keep six, dinner is eaten standing up, Ansel files Family v. Facilities, and Rule Four puts Pip on the Stool over her own father for her first grown-up case.
4. **Aisle Six.** A litigant corners Renata by the cereal and asks for a ruling she cannot give, while at home Ansel finds the word "precedent" in the Binder and moves to reopen every case he lost last year.
5. **The Back Door.** A kid from Hub Court brings the Pinwheel's first outside case to the back door, Renata rules that the court is family only, and the Full Table overrules her six forks to one.
6. **Order.** Ref's whistle goes missing, the family tries to have an ordinary argument without it and finds out it no longer knows how, and the whistle turns up in the freezer, clipped under DOCKET, filed as evidence by Pip.
7. **Across the River.** Renata is asked to sit on a bigger court across the river, tells only Wendell, and Wendell writes it on a docket slip by accident, so the whole house reads it before dinner.
