# The Returns Desk. Private adult-only sizzle. 60-second cut.

Target 60 seconds. Allowed range 55 to 70 seconds. Script only. No voice or video generated for this draft.

This is not the public trailer. It does not replace the locked 120-second production package (`projects/04-writers-room/out/production/L1-the-returns-desk-package.md`) or the 120-second shooting script (`scripts/L1-the-returns-desk-trailer-v2.md`). It is a shorter, adult-only cut for private review, built the same way as the 80-second draft supplied with this request: every line below is a locked line from the 120-second script, reassigned to its original adult speaker. No new dialogue is written. No child dialogue is reassigned or synthesized.

Cast in this cut: MARGUERITE SALCEDO, 63. AMBROSE QUAYLE, 74. DESMOND NUNES, 41, the customer.

Desmond Nunes is not a new character. He is named and screened as part of the locked 120-second script (`scripts/L1-the-returns-desk-trailer-v2.md`, cast list). No further name screening was needed for this cut.

Nieves Ruiz, Teddy Vance, and Dolores Ruiz are omitted, matching the 80-second draft's own choice. Their scenes carry the porch clue and the blank-tag conversation, so this cut drops both. It is not a fair-clue cut. Do not describe it as one.

## Format notes

- No music bed. Room tone only: fluorescent hum, quiet.
- Every effect (doorbell chime, front-door bell) sits at or below the loudest line of dialogue in the cut.
- One doorbell ring only, two soft notes, indicator glows once. No second ring, no strobe.
- Export target: -16 LUFS, no higher than -1 dBTP.
- No time-stretching dialogue and no repeated clips to hit the runtime. If a natural, unhurried read lands at 65 to 70 seconds, use that cut, not a stretched 60.
- The known image defects logged in this cut's own `review-notes.md` (house-number artifact on the shelf prop, Marguerite's pencil side drifting between angles, the doorbell indicator shown unlit in the `06-doorbell.png` reference, the tape not visibly gripping the button, faint marks on the blank receipt) are unresolved. This script does not certify those stills. The 120-second package's 12-point quality gate has not passed for this cut, and no video generation is approved at this stage.

## 00:00–00:08: It rings

Insert, close on the counter: the wireless doorbell, its button taped to a scrap of cardboard, a receipt folded into eighths. No establishing shot. Quiet room tone.

**MARGUERITE, offscreen:** Returns?

**DESMOND, offscreen:** It rings.

**MARGUERITE:** They do.

**DESMOND:** When nobody's there.

A beat. The indicator glows once. Two soft chime notes, no louder than the last line. Cut to Desmond, waiting for her reaction.

## 00:08–00:24: Store policy

Marguerite writes a tag. Desmond stands opposite in his raincoat over pajamas, tired and reasonable. She takes him seriously.

**MARGUERITE:** Hardison's. Ambler Street. Open all night. Everything comes back eventually.

**MARGUERITE:** Store policy says receipt required. My policy says story required.

Desmond produces the folded receipt. It is the one thing he is sure of.

**DESMOND:** I have the receipt.

**MARGUERITE:** Then you're halfway.

Give him a moment to process this. The front-door bell bridges the cut.

## 00:24–00:39: The next customer

Ambrose Quayle takes the customer mark, cap in hand, flashlight upright. Desmond stays nearby, outside this tighter framing. Quayle reports the problem sincerely.

**MR. QUAYLE:** I'd like to return this flashlight. It's showing me things.

**MARGUERITE:** What things?

**MR. QUAYLE:** Mostly the garage. But at night.

He sets down his receipt and smooths it flat.

**MR. QUAYLE:** I have the receipt. It's for a rake. I feel it's the same spirit.

Marguerite listens. No knowing smile, wink, or laugh track.

## 00:39–00:52: Two receipts

Desmond has the lid back on his shoebox. He looks at the shelf, then at Marguerite.

**DESMOND:** So is it haunted or not? I have to tell my wife something.

**MARGUERITE:** Do you have the receipt?

**DESMOND:** It's haunted.

**MARGUERITE:** Then I'll need two receipts.

Deliver the last line as ordinary store business. Do not add another punchline.

## 00:52–00:55: A place that listens

Hold on Desmond, at least one second. His jaw unclenches, one breath out. Hold on Marguerite, at least one second, as she takes a fresh tag. The customer has been heard, even though the question is not answered.

## 00:55–01:00: Title

Lamp click. The shelf darkens; the pale tags stay visible. Static type, no animation:

THE RETURNS DESK

Lights Out

Streetlights

The account holder is a parent.

No premiere date, no music, no call to action. Final half-second fade.

## What was cut from the 80-second draft, and why

Three cuts bring 80 seconds down to a 60-second target, each a trim of connective business, not a new joke or a dropped payoff:

1. The "Is that on the sign?" / "It's on the shelf" exchange is cut. It is a secondary joke on top of the "everything comes back" line, and the cut does not need two.
2. The reaction hold after Quayle's "same spirit" line is tightened from a beat to nothing; the front-door bell for his entrance already carries the transition.
3. The closing holds on Desmond and Marguerite shrink from at least two seconds each to at least one second each. The title card shrinks by about a second. Neither the "two receipts" line nor its landing is touched.

Every line that survives is unchanged from the 120-second locked script and the 80-second draft. The runtime note above still applies: treat 60 seconds as an edit target, not a forced stretch.
