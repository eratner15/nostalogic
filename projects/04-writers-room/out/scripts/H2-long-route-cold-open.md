# LONG ROUTE. Episode 101, "Timed." Cold open.

Hangout slot (Slot 2). Pilot cold open, about two and a half pages. Ends on a hard cut into the title.
Staff-writer output, Phase 4. Format: Fountain conventions.

```
FADE IN:

INT. COUNTY BUS BARN. BUS 12. DAWN.

A full-size yellow school bus. Engine ticking as it warms. A
stenciled 12 on the door. Twelve rows of green vinyl with
cracked piping.

Row 1, driver side: a steel thermos, a clipboard, a coffee can
of pencils. Row 1, door side: HOLLIS DUKES, 13, gray hood half
up, both hands on the seat back in front of him. Braced.

The bus is not moving.

FRANCES DUKES, 58, in the driver's seat, runs a finger down a
laminated route card she has not needed in thirty years.

                         FRANCES
          You can let go. We're parked.

                         HOLLIS
          I know.

He does not let go.

The radio crackles. LORNA (V.O.), dispatch. A voice like a
screen door.

                         LORNA (V.O.)
          Twelve. You've got a passenger.

                         FRANCES
          I've got Hollis.

                         LORNA (V.O.)
          A county passenger.

                         FRANCES
          Since when does the county ride?

                         LORNA (V.O.)
          Since they bought a stopwatch.

The door folds open. MR. TULL, 50s, tie, county lanyard, a
stopwatch on a cord around his neck, climbs the steps like
the steps might be inspected too.

                         TULL
          Ms. Keel. Tull. County
          transportation. Don't mind me.

                         FRANCES
          Sit anywhere.

                         HOLLIS
               (quiet)
          Not anywhere.

Tull starts down the aisle. Hollis watches him pass row 2.
Row 3, the heater seat. Row 7, the hump. Row 9, the Tuesday
window. Hollis rises one inch off his seat per row.

                         HOLLIS (CONT'D)
          Sorry. She means the front. She
          means... sorry.

Tull sits in row 12, driver side. The back row. He crosses
his legs. He looks pleased with the legroom.

Hollis turns to the wide convex mirror above the windshield.
Frances's eyes are already in it.

                         HOLLIS (CONT'D)
          He's in the back.

                         FRANCES
          I see him.

                         HOLLIS
          That's Mateo's.

                         FRANCES
          I see him.

                         HOLLIS
          On the first day.

                         FRANCES
          Hollis. I have a mirror.

                         LORNA (V.O.)
          Twelve, go.

                         FRANCES
          Going.

The bus moves. Hollis lets go of the seat. Then grabs it
again.


EXT. PENNOCK HOLLOW LANE. DAWN.

Gravel. Fence line. Four farms and a view. The bus stops at
the top of a long lane with its engine running. At the end of
the lane, the dairy house.

On its porch: ABUELA VILLANUEVA, 70s, a mug in one hand. At
the mailbox: INES VILLANUEVA, 6, in a backpack bigger than she
is, waving with her entire body.

MATEO VILLANUEVA, 14, comes down the lane at the exact walk of
someone who is not hurrying for a bus. Green work jacket.
Boots. A key ring in his hand with no keys on it.


INT. BUS 12. CONTINUOUS.

The bus does not move. Tull checks the stopwatch. Looks out
the window. Looks at the stopwatch.

                         TULL
          We're stopped.

                         HOLLIS
          Waiting on the wave.

                         TULL
          The boy's right there.

                         HOLLIS
          Not his wave.

Through the windshield, Ines waves harder. Mateo passes her.

                         INES
          Section one. Board with respect.

                         MATEO
          You're not on the bus, Ines.

                         INES
          Not yet.

Mateo climbs the steps. On the porch, Abuela raises her free
hand. Once. That is the wave. Frances lifts two fingers off
the wheel in answer.

Mateo comes up the aisle without looking, already turning
sideways to drop into row 12, and stops.

A man in a tie is in his seat.

Mateo says nothing. He turns around and finds the mirror.
Frances's eyes.

                         MATEO
          Frances.

                         FRANCES
          Mr. Tull. County.

                         MATEO
          He's in the back.

                         FRANCES
          Rule one. Everybody rides.

                         MATEO
          There's a code.

                         FRANCES
          Priya's not on till Fieldstone.

                         MATEO
          I'm not saying I care.

He sits in row 11, driver side. Directly in front of Tull. He
faces forward. He puts a knee up. He takes it down. There is
no way to sit in row 11 like you meant to.

The bus moves. Tull clicks the stopwatch.

                         TULL
          First day?

                         MATEO
          Last year. Eighth grade.

                         TULL
          Big year.

                         MATEO
          My cousin's getting a car. I'm off
          this thing by harvest.

                         TULL
          Then you won't mind.

                         MATEO
          Mind what?

Tull holds up the stopwatch.

                         TULL
          Route 12 is the longest route in
          the county. I'm timing it. Every
          stop. Both directions. All week.

                         MATEO
          It's not a long route. It's the
          whole way. There's a difference.

                         TULL
          The county doesn't have a column
          for that.

Mateo looks at the window. Then back.

                         MATEO
          What happens if it runs over?

                         TULL
          Then the county cuts what runs
          over.

Mateo looks out his window. The lane going away. The mailbox.
Ines, still waving, at the back of a bus that cannot see her.

Row 1. Hollis grabs the seat back.

                         HOLLIS
          Left. Left. Big one.

The bus drops. Everyone lifts an inch. The stopwatch leaves
Tull's hand, hangs in the air, and lands in Mateo's lap.

Mateo looks down at it. It is still running.

                         MATEO
          It didn't stop.

                         TULL
          It doesn't.

Mateo hands it back over his shoulder without turning around.

The mirror. Frances's eyes. She reaches up and touches the
visor, where the corner of a county envelope shows. She puts
her hand back on the wheel.

HARD CUT TO:

TITLE: LONG ROUTE
```

## Season 1 loglines, episodes 2 to 7

2. **The Quiet.** The twins split on a vote for the first time in their lives, the fight has to be held in total silence at the rail crossing, and Tola seconds the wrong motion on purpose to see whether Dele notices.
3. **Position Six.** The county moves Bus 12 from Position 3 to Position 6 in the school loop, and the six run a campaign through the open door that pulls in half of Consolidated before the crossing guard gives the signal.
4. **Harvest Seats.** Frances reassigns every seat for orchard season, Cal's ledger collapses under a hundred voided trades, and Priya gets row 5 for one week and is unbearable about it.
5. **Stop Zero.** Hollis asks for a stop of his own at the mailbox one before the barn, Frances grants it without a word, and the county adds the extra stop to Mr. Tull's clock.
6. **Wrong Order.** Frances is out for an orchestra audition the kids only hear about on the radio, Dale subs and drives the route backwards, and Hollis has to navigate for a man who will not take directions from row 1.
7. **Chains.** The first snow runs the ride double, a full session of parliament passes eleven motions and repeals ten, and the kids find out Mr. Tull is not the enemy because he rode a long route once too.
