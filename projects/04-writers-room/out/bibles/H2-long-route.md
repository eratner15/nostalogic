# LONG ROUTE

Series bible. Hangout slot (Slot 2). Tween and teen ensemble comedy. 22 minutes per episode. Live action, one set.

Written by the showrunner from the H2 tournament concept, the judge notes in `projects/04-writers-room/out/tournament/results.md`, and the Orchestrator note in `DECISIONS.md` D-020: keep school on screen through the windows and the stops, not only retold, and fight the "six kids in the same seats every week" problem head on.

---

## 1. Title, logline, pitch

**Title.** Long Route.

**Logline.** Six kids ride the county's last long school bus route for 48 minutes twice a day, and the bus is where everything that happens to them actually happens.

**Pitch a parent can repeat.** There is a school bus that goes all the way out to the orchards and back. Six kids ride it, and they have turned it into a tiny country: assigned seats, a written code, a back-row parliament, and a market for the good window. The driver, Frances, has driven it for thirty years and pretends not to listen. School is out the window and at the door. Home is at the end of the lane. The ride is the only place these six are all together, and they will never admit it is the best part of the day.

## 2. World

### 2.1 The route

The town has one consolidated school and a county bus barn. Most routes are short. Route 12 is not. It runs from the bus barn at the edge of town out past the crossroads store, over the rail crossing, across the creek bridge, past the orchards, up to the far end of Pennock Hollow, and back the same way to school. The depot board calls it Route 12. Everyone else calls it the Long Route.

The route has two directions and two moods. The morning ride collects the kids from the far end inward. Mateo boards first and rides longest. Priya boards last and rides shortest. The afternoon ride is the reverse. Nobody gets home early.

The Long Route is the shared town's countryside. The other three shows live in its streets. This show lives on the road out. Setting crosses freely under the canon crossover rule, so a Long Route detour through town may pass the porches and storefronts of the other shows without any character crossing.

### 2.2 The stops

Each stop is a place with its own rule and its own faces. Stops are numbered in morning order. Boarding order sets seat rank, so stop numbers matter.

**Stop 0. The bus barn.**
Rule: the bus does not leave until Lorna on dispatch says "Twelve, go." Faces: Lorna, the dispatcher, never seen, only heard on the radio. Dale Pruitt, driver of the Short Route (Route 4), Frances's rival in everything. Hollis Dukes boards here, because Frances is his grandmother and he rides with her from the barn. He would rather have a stop.

**Stop 1. Pennock Hollow, the far end.**
The Villanueva dairy, at the top of a long gravel lane. Rule: the bus waits for the wave. Mateo's abuela waves from the porch every morning and the bus does not move until she does. Faces: Abuela Villanueva, who waves. Ines Villanueva, age 6, Mateo's sister, who is not old enough to ride and stands at the mailbox every morning to prove she is ready. The far end is the show's named neighborhood. It is four farms, a lane, and a view.

**Stop 2. Hale Orchard stand.**
The Oyelaran family orchard. The stand has a chalkboard that faces the road. Rule: no fruit boards the bus unless it is paid for or gifted on the board. Faces: Adaeze Oyelaran, the twins' mother, who writes the board and is the funniest person on the route the kids never hear. The orchard dog, Sergeant, who chases the bus to the property line and stops exactly there.

**Stop 3. The creek bridge.**
Not a stop. A slow. The bridge is one lane and the bus crawls. Rule: everyone looks left and someone gives the Creek Report: high, low, frozen, or "there's the heron." Faces: the heron. Any kid who gives a wrong Creek Report is corrected for a week.

**Stop 4. The rail crossing.**
By law the bus stops, opens the door, and listens. The kids call it the Quiet. Rule: total silence until Frances closes the door. Anything said during the Quiet does not count. Nothing on the route is funnier than six kids holding a fight for eleven seconds with their faces.

**Stop 5. Whitlock's Corner, the crossroads store.**
Cal Whitlock's family store, at the only four-way stop on the route. Rule: Pop Whitlock hands Frances her coffee through the door and Frances says "Obliged" and nothing else. Faces: Pop Whitlock, who knows everyone's business and prices it. The store's letter-board sign, which changes weekly and is often wrong in a way that starts a story.

**Stop 6. The ridge.**
Not a stop. The Look. The road tops the ridge and the whole town is visible for one curve. Phones get signal here and only here. Rule: nobody reads the ridge out loud. Whatever a phone says at the ridge, it waits until the loop.

**Stop 7. Fieldstone.**
The newest subdivision at the edge of town. The bus cannot turn in the cul-de-sac, so Priya walks to the entrance sign. Rule: the bus waits until a parent is visible. Faces: Priya's dad, Marcus Coker, doing the crossword in the car. Otis Bramley, age 9, who rides the Short Route and treats the Long Route kids as celebrities.

**Stop 8. The school loop.**
North Union Consolidated, kindergarten through eighth grade. Everyone calls it Consolidated. The bus has Position 3 in the loop and defends it. Rule: nobody stands until the door opens and Mr. Ferrante, the crossing guard, gives the signal. Faces: Mr. Ferrante. Ms. Odum, the teacher on bus duty, who comes to the door with news. Principal Vashti Greer, who comes to the door with worse news. Kids from other routes, seen through the glass.

### 2.3 The school seen from the bus

The Orchestrator note is the design rule: school is on screen, not retold. Four devices carry it.

1. **The loop.** Every episode has at least one loop scene: the bus sits at Consolidated with the door open, and school comes to the door. Teachers hand things in. The principal asks questions. Other kids ask to see the back row. A tuba needs a ride. The four-minute wait in the loop is a stage.
2. **The windshield.** From Position 3 the bus sees the marquee sign with its missing letters, the gym doors, the eighth-grade window where Mateo's class is, and the playground where Ines will be next year. Things happen in those windows during the loop scene and the kids react through glass.
3. **Rue's notebook.** Rue draws what she saw through the school windows that day. The drawings are on screen. They are the show's way to show a school day in one image without a classroom set.
4. **Field trips and stunts.** Frances drives the field trips. When the whole school rides, the whole school is on the set. The science fair volcano rides to the county. The eighth grade rides to the high school. Picture day comes to the loop.

### 2.4 The seasons of the route

The road plates change and so does the ride.

- **Harvest.** The orchard stand is full. Seats are reassigned for the stand traffic. The board sells apples by the bag and the twins are embarrassed by their own crop.
- **Rain.** Mud at the far end. The creek bridge floods. The route detours through town for the first time, and the kids see the town from the bus.
- **Snow.** Chains. The ride runs double. Parliament holds full sessions. The heater seat becomes currency.
- **Thaw.** The creek is high. The Creek Report is a daily bulletin. The heron comes back.
- **Blossom.** The orchard is pretty and the twins hate it. The last ride runs in reverse order.

### 2.5 The bus set, so an artist can draw it

The bus is a full-size county school bus, yellow, with a black stripe and a stenciled 12 on both sides and the rear door. Inside: twelve rows, two seats per row, a center aisle, green vinyl seats with cracked piping, chrome grab rails on the seat backs, black rubber floor with a raised strip down the aisle, a heater box under row 3 on the door side, wheel humps under rows 6 and 7, and a rear emergency door with a red handle nobody touches.

Row 1 is behind Frances. On the driver side, Frances's things: a thermos, a clipboard, a laminated route card, a coffee can of pencils, and on some days an upright bass in a canvas case, seat-belted in. On the door side, Hollis. The kids call row 1 "the kitchen."

The six assigned seats and why:

| Rider | Seat | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Hollis | Row 1, door side | Boards at the barn. Sits where his grandmother can reach him. Hates it. |
| Mateo | Row 12, driver side (the back row) | Boards first. Rode alone for twenty minutes a day for three years before the others. Speaker of the parliament. |
| Beck | Row 12, door side (the back row) | Boards second with Rue. Took the other back-row seat by winning a vote he ran himself. |
| Rue | Row 11, door side | Boards second with Beck. Chose the row in front of the back row. Says it is not a political statement. |
| Cal | Row 7, driver side (the wheel hump) | Boards third. Took the hump because it is the middle of the market and he can see both ends. |
| Priya | Row 2, door side | Boards last. Assigned the front by Frances because she is the youngest. Her whole want is a seat farther back. |

Seats with value in the seat economy: the heater seat (row 3, door side, warm in winter), the hump seats (rows 6 and 7, feet up), the seat with the good latch (row 9, door side, the only window that opens smooth, called the Tuesday window because Mateo once let Priya have it on a Tuesday and it became a precedent), the crack seat (row 9, driver side, a split in the vinyl that holds folded notes), and the back row.

**The back row.** Two seats and the aisle between them. The parliament sits here in session. Rule from Frances: the back row is for business, and everyone returns to their seat before the ridge.

**The driver's mirror.** A wide convex mirror above the windshield. Frances sees the whole aisle in it, fisheye. The kids see her eyes in it. Half the show's best lines are said to the mirror.

**Where the camera lives.** Three fixed positions and one moving.
1. The mirror shot: a wide, slightly bent view down the aisle from above Frances, as if through the mirror. The show's signature frame. Every kid in one image.
2. The stairwell shot: from the door steps looking back up the aisle, low, at kid height.
3. The seat rigs: one fixed frame per assigned seat, each kid framed against their own window with the road moving behind them. Six frames. These are the show's portrait shots, and the road plates behind them change with the stop and the season.
4. The door shot: from inside the bus looking out the open door at whatever the stop brings.
A handheld covers the back row in session.

**Road plates.** Every window is a plate. The plate library is organized by stop and by season: far-end lane, orchard, creek bridge, rail crossing, crossroads, ridge, Fieldstone, loop, and town detour, each in harvest, rain, snow, thaw, and blossom. The plate outside a window tells the viewer where on the route we are without a line of dialogue.

## 3. Characters

Seven regulars: six kids and one driver. All original. None modeled on any character from any existing show.

### Frances Dukes, 58. The driver.

**Want.** A quiet route, and to see these six through to the last day of eighth grade.
**Flaw.** She cannot stop listening, and she cannot leave a problem unsolved past the ridge.
**Funny about.** She has a rule for everything and the kids have never learned how many rules there are. She numbers them out of order on purpose. She solves things with the door, the mirror, and one word.
**How she speaks.** To the mirror. Short. Dry. Rules stated as weather.
- "I'm not listening. I'm driving. I just drive with my ears."
- "Rule nineteen. No motions about a person. Rule four. Sit down, Beck."
- "Obliged." (To Pop Whitlock, every morning, about the coffee, and never a word more.)
**The life the kids only glimpse.** She plays upright bass in the community orchestra and some mornings the bass has a seat. She and Lorna on dispatch have a radio friendship that is thirty years old and mostly insults. She and Dale Pruitt race the Short Route to the loop by rules nobody else knows. Her daughter Denise, Hollis's mother, works nights, which is why Hollis rides from the barn. There is a letter from the county in her visor with the word "retire" in it, and she has not opened it. She was a rider on this route once. She knows which seat.
**What the parent sees.** The adult who is neither clueless nor the enemy. Competent, tired, funny on purpose, running a small nation from a driver's seat. The secret lead.
**What the kid sees.** The adult who pretends not to listen and always hears. The one you can push, who never breaks, and who never leaves anyone at a stop.

### Mateo Villanueva, 14. Stop 1. The back row, driver side. Speaker of the parliament.

**Want.** A ride in anything that is not this bus. His cousin is getting a car. He talks about it daily.
**Flaw.** He loves the bus and would rather be dropped at the creek than say so.
**Funny about.** Denial. He runs parliament like it is beneath him and cares about the Tuesday window more than anyone. He has rules of his own, older than Priya's code, written in crayon, and he denies those too.
**How he speaks.** Dismissals that give him away.
- "The day my cousin gets that car I'm gone. Anyway, who's got the window Tuesday?"
- "I don't run parliament. Parliament runs. I sit near it."
- "It's not a long route. It's the whole way. There's a difference."
**What the parent sees.** Their own fourteen-year-old self, pretending not to need the thing they need.
**What the kid sees.** The oldest kid on the bus, who is in charge without trying, and who might let you in.

### Priya Anand-Coker, 11. Stop 7. Row 2, door side. Author of the bus code.

**Want.** A seat farther back, and the respect of the back row.
**Flaw.** She trusts rules more than people, on a bus full of people who negotiate everything.
**Funny about.** Citations. She wrote the bus code in a composition book, with sections and amendments, and she can quote it faster than anyone can break it. She is the youngest and the most formal person on the route.
**How she speaks.** In sections.
- "Section 3 says no standing. I wrote section 3. Sit."
- "There's a bus code." "You wrote the bus code." "So you know it's good."
- "I would like the record to show I was right at the creek and nobody said so."
**What the parent sees.** The kid who reads the whole form. The kid who will be a judge, or a clerk, or the one who knows where the fire extinguisher is.
**What the kid sees.** The one who wrote the rules and will use them for you if you ask nicely.

### Beck Oyelaran, 12. Stop 2. The back row, door side. The whip.

**Want.** To be Speaker. Mateo has the job and does not want it, which is unbearable.
**Flaw.** Cannot lose a vote. Demands a recount of everything, including things that were not votes.
**Funny about.** Parliamentary procedure as theater. He makes motions about the weather. He stands to speak, which breaks section 3, which starts a trial, which he treats as a campaign.
**How he speaks.** Speeches. Always standing when he should be sitting.
- "I move that the creek was high. Second? Anyone? Rue. Rue, second it."
- "The count was four to two. I request a recount. I request a recount of the recount."
- "I'm not loud. The bus is quiet. That's a bus problem."
**What the parent sees.** The kid who will run for everything and someday should.
**What the kid sees.** The loudest, funniest person in the back, who makes every ride an event.

### Rue Oyelaran, 12. Stop 2. Row 11, door side. The swing vote.

**Want.** To be seen as her own person and not half of "the twins."
**Flaw.** Says the true thing at the exact wrong moment.
**Funny about.** Deadpan. She draws what she sees out the window in a notebook, keeps the parliament's minutes, and breaks the twin bloc when nobody expects it. Beck sometimes does not notice for days.
**How she speaks.** One line, late, accurate.
- "I seconded it because you were going to keep saying my name."
- "Minutes of the ride: Beck stood. Priya cited. Nothing passed. The heron was there."
- "We're not a bloc. We're two people who get on at the same stop."
**What the parent sees.** The quiet kid who is the smartest person in the room and lets the room find out slowly.
**What the kid sees.** The one who draws the school through the window and sees what really happened.

### Cal Whitlock, 13. Stop 5. Row 7, driver side. The market.

**Want.** To turn the bus into a business. He keeps a ledger of every seat trade, snack loan, and homework barter on the route.
**Flaw.** He will trade away something he loves for a deal that sounds good at the store.
**Funny about.** He prices everything. Seat futures. Window options. Snack credit. The heater seat has a winter rate. He speaks in offers and closes deals at the ridge.
**How he speaks.** In terms.
- "Heater seat, three rides, one granola bar and you carry my project up the loop. Final."
- "I don't have a seat. I have a position."
- "The ledger doesn't lie. The ledger is missing. Those are different problems."
**What the parent sees.** The kid who will run the store, or something bigger, and the kid who has to learn what not to sell.
**What the kid sees.** The guy who can get you anything before the ridge.

### Hollis Dukes, 13. Stop 0. Row 1, door side. The driver's grandson.

**Want.** A stop of his own, like a normal kid. He rides from the bus barn with his grandmother, in the kitchen seat, and it mortifies him.
**Flaw.** He apologizes for Frances. Constantly. She does not need it.
**Funny about.** He knows every pothole on the route by name and braces for each one before it hits. He can fix the door with a pencil. He speaks in tiny sentences and his body does the rest. His mortification is physical comedy.
**How he speaks.** As few words as possible.
- "Left. Left. Big one." (Before every pothole. He is never wrong.)
- "She's not listening. She's driving. Sorry. She says that. Sorry."
- "I'd like to be dropped at the mailbox. One before the barn. So I can walk in. Like a person."
**What the parent sees.** The kid raised partly by a grandparent, loved out loud, wishing it were quieter. The parent laughs because they have been Frances and been Hollis both.
**What the kid sees.** The one who knows the bus better than anyone and is stuck up front with the grown-up.

### Recurring faces (not regulars)

- **Lorna.** Dispatch. Voice only. Frances's oldest friend and sparring partner.
- **Dale Pruitt.** Short Route driver. Frances's rival. The substitute when she is out. Drives the route in the wrong order.
- **Abuela Villanueva.** Waves. The bus waits.
- **Ines Villanueva, 6.** Mateo's sister. Not yet a rider. Learning the code from the mailbox. The show's six-year-old on screen.
- **Adaeze Oyelaran.** Writes the orchard chalkboard.
- **Pop Whitlock.** Hands the coffee through the door.
- **Marcus Coker.** Priya's dad. Does the crossword at the Fieldstone sign.
- **Otis Bramley, 9.** Short Route kid who wants to ride the Long Route more than anything.
- **Mr. Ferrante.** Crossing guard. Gives the signal.
- **Ms. Odum.** Teacher on bus duty. Comes to the door with news.
- **Principal Vashti Greer.** Comes to the door with worse news.
- **Mr. Tull.** The county's transportation man. Rides with a stopwatch in season 1. Comes back without one.

## 4. Engine

### 4.1 The ride as a clock

Every episode is a ride. Some are a morning ride. Some are an afternoon ride. Some are both, with the school day in between seen through the loop and the windows. The ride is 48 minutes long and the episode is 22, so the episode always cuts, but the geography holds: the story moves in stop order, and the audience always knows where on the route it is by what is outside the window.

**Morning ride shape.** Barn (Hollis and Frances, the setup). Far end (Mateo boards, the wave). Orchard (the twins board, the chalkboard). Creek (the Creek Report). Rail crossing (the Quiet). Crossroads (Cal boards, the coffee). Ridge (phones wake, the Look). Fieldstone (Priya boards, the code arrives). Loop (school comes to the door).

**Afternoon ride shape.** The loop in reverse: kids board in the order their classes let out, so the seat plan is contested every afternoon. Fieldstone (Priya off, the first loss). Ridge (the last signal). Crossroads. Rail crossing. Creek. Orchard. Far end (Mateo off, the last stop, the wave home). Barn (Frances and Hollis alone, the show's quietest scene).

The morning ride is about what is going to happen. The afternoon ride is about what did. The route runs both directions, so the show has a built-in setup and payoff.

### 4.2 The parliament

The back row calls itself a parliament. It has a Speaker (Mateo, who denies it), a whip (Beck), a clerk who keeps minutes (Rue), a treasurer of sorts (Cal), and a written code (Priya's composition book). Any rider can bring a motion. Motions are about seats, windows, the Creek Report, the order of boarding in the afternoon, whether a sneeze during the Quiet counts, and what to do about a wasp. The code forbids motions about a person. Frances's rule nineteen backs the code. Parliament is never used against anyone. It is used about things.

Parliament is the kid-side engine. Every episode has one session, and the session is where the six argue about the thing the episode is really about.

### 4.3 The stop of the week

Every episode, one stop brings the outside in. A stop's rule bends, a stop's face boards, or a stop changes. The orchard chalkboard says something wrong. Pop Whitlock is late with the coffee. The rail gate sticks. The heron is gone. The county repaints the stop signs. Fieldstone gets a second kid. Ms. Odum comes to the door with a tuba. The stop of the week is how the world enters a show that is set on one bus, and it is the answer to the sameness problem: the bus is the same, and what comes through the door is not.

### 4.4 The seat economy

Seats have value and value changes. The heater seat is worth more in snow. The Tuesday window is worth more in blossom. The hump seats are worth more with a heavy backpack. Cal keeps the ledger. Priya keeps the code. Frances keeps the veto. The seat economy gives every ride a small stake, and it lets a kid win something on their own logic every week.

### 4.5 How it yields 100 episodes

Two rides a day, one school year, six kids, eight stops, five seasons of road, and one driver with a life. Multiply any two: a kid and a stop, a stop and a season, a season and a rule. The seeds in section 8 come from those pairs without strain. The room's test is that the seed can be stated as "the ride where X," and every seed below can.

### 4.6 How it fights sameness

The judges' worry is right, so the show is built against it. Ten levers.

1. **Windows.** The road plates change with every stop and every season. No two episodes have the same view out the same window.
2. **The loop.** School is at the door in every episode. Teachers, the principal, other kids, props, and news enter through the stairwell.
3. **Stops.** One stop changes per episode. Its face, its rule, or its sign.
4. **Weather.** Fog blanks the windows. Snow doubles the ride. Rain floods the bridge. Frost fogs the glass from the inside.
5. **Substitutes.** Dale drives the route backwards. A by-the-card sub follows a wrong card. Hollis must navigate.
6. **Breakdowns.** The heater dies. The door sticks. The bus stops at the orchard and the morning is run from the driver's seat with dispatch on the radio.
7. **Field trips.** Forty kids on the bus. The six defend their seats from strangers. The whole school is the set for a day.
8. **Route changes.** The creek bridge closes and the route runs through town. The county adds a fair-grounds stop for a week. The stop signs are renumbered.
9. **Seat changes.** Harvest reassignment. The last-day swap. A new mirror that makes the back row visible. A goat that will not decide.
10. **Ride direction.** Morning episodes, afternoon episodes, and both-ride episodes have different shapes. The afternoon boarding order is a contest every time.

## 5. Tone and comedy

**Character first.** The bus is never the joke. The people are. Priya's code, Mateo's denial, Beck's motions, Rue's timing, Cal's ledger, Hollis's bracing, and Frances's mirror are the seven engines. A situation is only funny here because of who is in it.

**Tone band.** Quick, loud, loyal. Friends against the afternoon, and against the county. Competence humor: each of the six is good at something odd, and the show respects it. Small, funny, and honest about being eleven.

**Three rules the room follows.**
1. The outside comes in. Every episode has a loop scene and a stop of the week. If a draft has six kids talking in their seats for the whole ride, it goes back.
2. Every kid is right at least once. Whoever loses the vote wins the ride somewhere else, on their own logic.
3. Nobody is stranded. Every problem resolves by the last stop or is handed to Frances, on screen, before the door closes.

**What the show never does.**
- Never mocks the country, the farms, the orchard, or the far end. Rural is the setting, not the punchline.
- Never makes the bus scary. No dark rides, no menace, no "left behind."
- Never has an adult villain. The county man is a man with a stopwatch, and by the finale he wants to see the heron.
- Never has a motion about a person. Parliament is never a weapon.
- Never makes a phone the punchline. The ridge is the only signal and that is a fact of the road, not a joke about kids.
- Never tells a school day instead of showing one at the door or through the glass.

Per scene type: the laugh for the parent and the laugh for the kid. Neither depends on the other.

| Parliament in session | Real procedure applied to a window seat. The recount of the recount. | Beck standing. Priya citing section 3. Rue's late second. |
| The seat economy | Cal's terms. Winter rates. "I don't have a seat. I have a position." | Winning the heater seat. Losing it to a granola bar. |
| The ridge | A whole bus of kids going silent to read one minute of signal and Frances's rule that nobody reads it out loud. | The one phone that lights up first. |

Soft rule 4 (one moment to talk about after) is designed in: the afternoon ride always contains a scene where someone admits the thing they denied in the morning. That is the conversation prompt in the ritual kit.

## 7. Season 1 arc

**Season question.** Does the Long Route survive the year?

The county has a limit on route length. Route 12 runs over it. Mr. Tull rides with a stopwatch in the pilot and the kids learn that the only way to save the ride they claim to hate is to make it shorter, which means losing the part of the day they actually want. Every episode moves the question a little. The answer lands in episode 10.

1. **Timed.** First day of the year. Mr. Tull from the county boards with a stopwatch and sits in a contested seat. Priya files a petition to move back. The route runs nine minutes over the limit.
2. **The Quiet.** The twins split on a vote for the first time, and the fight has to be held in silence at the rail crossing. Rue seconds the wrong motion on purpose.
3. **Position Six.** The bus is moved from Position 3 to Position 6 in the loop. The six campaign to get it back through the door, and half the school gets involved.
4. **Harvest Seats.** Frances reassigns seats for orchard season. Cal's ledger collapses. Priya gets row 5 for a week and is unbearable about it.
5. **Stop Zero.** Hollis asks for a stop of his own: the mailbox one before the barn. Frances grants it. The county notices the extra stop and adds it to the clock.
6. **Wrong Order.** Frances is out for the orchestra audition the kids only hear about. Dale subs and drives the route backwards. Hollis must navigate for a man who will not listen to him.
7. **Chains.** First snow. The ride runs double. A full session of parliament passes eleven motions and repeals ten. Mr. Tull's clock is now catastrophic, and the kids find out he is not the enemy: he rode a long route once too.
8. **The Detour.** The creek bridge closes. The route runs through town for the first time. The kids see the town from the bus. The detour is nine minutes shorter. The county likes it. The kids do not.
9. **Ines.** Mateo's sister gets a trial ride and sits in the back row and nobody dares move her. The county's proposal arrives: a van for Pennock Hollow. Mateo would ride alone. He says he wants that. Nobody believes him.
10. **Board Meeting on Wheels.** The county board rides the route to decide. Parliament presents. Priya cites. Beck stands. Rue reads the minutes of the year, one page. Mateo, who wanted out, is the one who says, out loud, in front of everyone, that it is not a long route, it is the whole way. The route stays. The bridge reopens. The last ride runs in reverse and Mateo gives Priya the Tuesday window without a vote.

**How it lands.** The route survives because a kid who spent ten episodes denying he loved it says so to a room full of adults. The season's last line is Frances's, to the mirror, with the door closed on the county: "Twelve, go."

## 8. 100 episode seeds

1. Priya moves to abolish assigned seats. Anarchy by the creek. Order restored by the ridge.

## 9. Standards pass

### Section 2, hard FAIL items

1. **Sexual content or romance beyond a first crush.** None. The show has no romance thread among the kids. If a crush ever appears it stops at a hand hold in the loop and never on the bus.
2. **Graphic violence, weapons, injury for realism.** None. The wasp is the most dangerous thing on the route and nobody swats it. Potholes are called, not suffered.
3. **Profanity or substitute words.** None. Frances's strongest word is "Obliged." Beck's is "Recount."

Frances's rule nineteen backs it. Parliament is only ever about things. Seed 86, the "driver's baby" line from a Short Route kid, is written so the story answers it three times: by the kids, by Frances, and by Dale, the other kid's own driver. No episode ends with a kid humiliated. Losing a vote is always paid back on the same ride.
6. **Adults who threaten children, or unsupervised danger for laughs.** Frances is present in every episode and in every scene set on the road. Snow (7, 59) is delay, never peril. No kid is ever alone on the road. The rule "nobody is stranded" is a room rule, not a hope. 8. **Real-world hate, politics, or religion as a target.** Parliament is procedure comedy, not politics. No party, no real office, no real issue. The county board is a transportation committee about a bus route. No religion appears.

### Section 3, soft rules

1. **Slot 4 scares resolve.** Not applicable.
2. **Child sarcasm to a parent lands as affection.** The kids' sarcasm is aimed at each other and at rules. Hollis's lines about Frances are mortification, not contempt, and the afternoon barn scene each episode shows the affection plainly.

## 10. IP distance statement

**What it could be mistaken for.** School-bus comedies, including animated shows with a memorable bus driver and any children's series set on a magical or talking bus. Kid ensemble sitcoms set around a school. Rural small-town comedies with a grandparent figure.

**It is none of those.** No existing series lives on a long rural route with a written kid code, a back-row parliament, a seat economy, and a driver-grandson pair, and no plot or character here is taken from one.

**Five concrete choices that keep it distinct.**
1. **No classroom set. No magic.** The school is only ever seen through the loop door and the windows. The bus never transforms, never talks, and never goes anywhere a bus cannot go.
2. **The Quiet.** The mandatory stop at the rail crossing as a silent ritual, where fights are held with faces, is an original device grounded in real bus law. It is the show's signature scene.
3. **A code written by the youngest rider.** Priya's composition-book bus code, with sections and amendments, is the show's constitution, and the fact that the youngest wrote it is the show's own joke.
4. **The driver as a parent-facing secret lead with a grandson in the kitchen seat.** Frances's mirror lines, her bass in row 1, her radio friendship with Lorna, and Hollis's mortification are a specific family, not an archetype.
5. **The mirror shot and the seat rigs.** The show's look is a fisheye down the aisle and six portrait frames against moving road plates. That visual grammar belongs to this show.

## 11. Production note

**One set.** A rented full-size school bus interior, dressed as in section 2.5, on a stage or a lot. Exteriors are road plates through the windows. The loop, the stops, and the door shots are the same bus with a different plate and a different face at the door. A second bus is not needed: the Short Route is heard on the radio and seen only as a plate.

**Cast size.** Seven regulars: six child performers and one adult. Recurring adults and Ines appear at the door and through the glass. Child performers trigger CG-04 (talent and guild agreements). Ines is a small role designed so a six-year-old performer works short days at the door and the mailbox only.

**What AI-assisted production can carry for the trailer.**
- The six fixed seat frames, each kid against a window with a road plate behind, because assigned seats lock each face into one framing.
- The road plate library: far-end lane, orchard, creek, rail crossing, crossroads, ridge, Fieldstone, loop, in the seasons the trailer needs.
- The mirror shot as a composite: a fisheye aisle plate with the seat frames placed in it.
- Exterior establishing shots: the bus on the orchard road at dawn, the bus in the loop, the bus at the crossing with the door open.
- The school seen from Position 3: marquee, gym doors, the eighth-grade window.

**What needs a human performance.**
- Frances's voice and her eyes in the mirror. The show's dry counterpoint does not survive synthesis, and any synthetic voice triggers CG-03 with disclosure.
- The Quiet. Six faces holding a fight in silence is comic timing, not a still.
- The parliament in session: overlapping dialogue and reaction, cut on the handheld.
- Priya's line readings. The code only works if the citations are fast and sincere.
Any AI-generated child likeness in released material requires the CG-03 gate and the disclosure, and the trailer plan should assume human child performers for dialogue scenes with AI-assisted plates and composites behind them.

## 12. Trailer brief for the staff-writer

90 seconds. Six beats per the block bible section 8. Dialogue only, no narration, no text cards. Trailer story is the pilot, "Timed."

**The question the trailer asks.** Will the county cut the Long Route?

**Beat 1. Cold open (0 to 12).**
A grown man in a tie sits in the back row of a school bus holding a stopwatch. Priya, small, beside him, composition book open. Priya: "You're in a contested seat." Mr. Tull: "I'm from the county." Priya: "Then you should know better." Cut on his face.

**Beat 2. World (12 to 30).**
Dawn on the orchard road. The bus at the top of the Pennock Hollow lane, not moving. Abuela waves. The bus moves. The orchard chalkboard through the window. The bus crawling the creek bridge, and a voice from inside: "There's the heron." The rail crossing: door open, six kids silent, Beck's face fighting someone in total quiet. Frances closes the door. Frances, to the mirror: "Rule of the crossing. That didn't count."

**Beat 3. Character (30 to 55).**
Back row in session. Mateo: "The day my cousin gets that car I'm gone. Anyway, who's got the window Tuesday?" Beck, standing: "I move that it's me." Priya, from the front, without turning around: "Section 3. No standing. I wrote section 3. Sit." Beck sits. Cal, from the hump: "Window's been sold. Twice." Hollis, in the kitchen seat, braced: "Left. Left. Big one." The bus drops. Everyone lifts an inch. Frances, to the mirror, dry: "I'm not listening. I'm driving. I just drive with my ears." (Parent laugh.)

**Beat 4. Turn (55 to 75).**
The loop. Ms. Odum at the door. Mr. Tull clicks the stopwatch and stands in the aisle. Mr. Tull: "Route 12 runs nine minutes over the county limit. Cut nine minutes or the county splits the route." Silence. Beck, small: "Which nine?" Mateo, looking out the window: "I said I wanted off." Rue: "You said it to the window." The turn is not the vote. The turn is that the kids can save the route only by making it shorter, and the ride is the thing they want.

**Beat 5. Button (75 to 85).**
Back row. Mr. Tull is still in the contested seat. Priya has moved next to him. Priya: "All in favor of the county sitting somewhere else." Six hands. Mr. Tull's hand goes up too, slowly. Priya: "Motion carries. Sir." The cold open now makes sense. (Kid laugh.)

**Beat 6. Title card (85 to 90).**
Over the door closing on the county man's stopwatch, Frances's last line, to the mirror: "Sit down, sir. We're not there yet." Title: LONG ROUTE. Slot label: Hangout. "Premieres Friday on [BRAND]." Small line: "The account holder is a parent." No call to action.

**The two laughs.**
- Parent: "I'm not listening. I'm driving. I just drive with my ears." A 35-year-old does not need context for a grown-up who has heard everything.
- Kid: The Quiet, Beck's silent fight face at the crossing, and the button where the county man votes himself out of a seat. Both land on sight.

**Tests to pass.** Question test: "Will the county cut the route?" Two-laugh test: above. Cold test: a man in a tie with a stopwatch in the back row of a school bus, and a small girl telling him he is in a contested seat. Dialogue test: every beat is a character speaking to a character; the world beat carries the Creek Report and the crossing rule as spoken lines. Standards test: no call to action, no brand on the bus, no scare, Frances present in every road shot.

**The last line.** Frances: "Sit down, sir. We're not there yet."
